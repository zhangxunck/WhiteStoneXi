一、喚醒

上一篇結尾我寫了一套系統：本地一個跑腿的，只探測、上報，不判斷；雲端一個審判的，全靜默直到被叫醒。

這一篇從那一秒開始。

假設某天早上，本地那個進程掃到一條標記為「提問」的事項。它不判斷、不回答、不寫任何結論。它做的事只有一件：寫下一個信號，把雲端那個從沉默裡叫醒。

雲端那個醒來，讀完全部材料，出一份研判。研判發回來，本地進程收到，照著執行。執行完，寫日誌，睡覺。

這套流程裡沒有「負責人」。 不是沒有人負責——人始終在環，是最高審批那一步永遠由人籤——而是這個系統沒有一個環節可以被問「這件事是誰決定的」。探測是程序做的，決定是模型做的，執行是程序做的，拍板是人做的，但沒有任何一個角色持有全局。

上一篇談的是一個名字為什麼成立。這一篇談的是：多個名字湊在一起之後，它們之間是什麼關係。

上一篇我說，系統裡的神名是一個減震器，為概率系統的不可預測性減震。但減震器有一個致命的前提：它只對一個黑盒起作用。當WALLe與Eva必須通過管道交換結構化指令時，神話的外殼瞬間就碎了——程序不相信神諭，程序只認狀態碼和信號。兩個名字湊在一起之後，神話不僅不再是減震器，反而成了掩蓋系統脆斷的噪音。

1. Waking

At the end of the previous essay I described a system: a local runner that probes and reports, but does not judge; a cloud judge, fully silent until woken.

This essay starts from that very second.

Suppose one morning the local process scans an item marked "question." It does not judge, does not answer, does not write any conclusion. It does one thing only: it writes a signal, waking the cloud one from silence.

The cloud one wakes, reads all the material, and produces an assessment. The assessment comes back. The local process receives it and executes it. When execution is done, it writes a log and goes back to sleep.

There is no "person responsible" in this flow. That does not mean no one is responsible — a human stays in the loop, and the highest approval step is always signed by a human — but no single link in this system can be asked, "Who decided this?" Probing is done by code, deciding is done by a model, executing is done by code, approving is done by a human, yet no single role holds the whole picture.

The previous essay asked why a name holds. This essay asks: once several names come together, what is the relationship between them?

In the previous essay I said the god name in the system is a shock absorber, damping the unpredictability of a probabilistic system. But the shock absorber has a fatal precondition: it only works on a single black box. When WALLe and Eva must exchange structured instructions through a pipeline, the shell of myth shatters in an instant — code does not believe in oracles; code recognizes only status codes and signals. Once two names come together, myth is no longer a shock absorber. It becomes noise hiding the system's brittle breaks.

二、主線：一座塔塌了

1978 年，英國管理學家查爾斯·漢迪（Charles Handy）出版了《眾神之神》（Gods of Management: The Changing Work of Organizations）。

這本書最常被引用的是一個分類表：他把組織文化分成四類，分別對應希臘四神——宙斯（權力/俱樂部，蛛網結構）、阿波羅（角色/神廟，柱式結構）、雅典娜（任務/攻堅，網格結構）、狄奧尼索斯（個體/存在，反向星座）。

但那個表不是這本書的核心。這本書的核心是一條敘事線（本文的重構性解讀，非原書章節結構），而這條線才是它對今天最有用的部分：

阿波羅建起了工業時代的科層金字塔。 一戰後到七十年代，流水線、大企業、職能分工，角色文化達到頂峰。那時候的組織信仰是：把人放在正確的格子裡，定義清楚他的職責、權限、彙報線，事情就會自動變好。

然後塔塌了。 七十年代石油危機，知識經濟起來，規則變化的速度超過了科層的更新能力。你花兩年建起來的流程架構，落地時市場已經變了。層級不再能保證效率，它變成了信息在向上傳遞的路上被一層層削掉、被一層層加進私貨的地方。

塌方之後，三神從廢墟裡站了起來。 權力慾極強的宙斯型（靠個人魅力和非正式網絡凝聚人）、能者居之的雅典娜型（圍繞問題臨時組隊）、以及根本不在乎組織的狄奧尼索斯型（我就是獨立專家，你們來請我）。

漢迪真正的洞察不在於「組織有四種文化」，在於這四種不是並列的選項，而是有興替關係的。阿波羅是主流，塌了之後另外三神才有機會。而阿波羅會在下一次知識複雜度上升時重新顯得可靠，然後再次塌掉。

這個循環，現在正在 AI 上重演。

2. The Main Line: A Tower Fell

In 1978, the British management thinker Charles Handy published Gods of Management: The Changing Work of Organizations.

The book is most often cited for a classification table: he divided organizational culture into four types, each mapped to a Greek god — Zeus (power / club, web structure), Apollo (role / temple, pillar structure), Athena (task / task force, grid structure), Dionysus (individual / existence, inverted constellation).

But that table is not the book's core. The book's core is a narrative line (this essay's reconstructive reading, not the original book's chapter structure), and that line is the part most useful to us today:

Apollo built the bureaucratic pyramid of the industrial age. From after World War I into the 1970s: assembly lines, big corporations, functional division of labor. Role culture peaked. The organizational faith of the time was: put people in the right boxes, define their duties, authority, and reporting lines clearly, and things will take care of themselves.

Then the tower fell. The 1970s oil crisis hit. The knowledge economy rose. Rules changed faster than bureaucracy could update. A process architecture that took two years to build landed in a market that had already moved. Hierarchy no longer guaranteed efficiency. It became the place where information was shaved away layer by layer on its way up, and where private agendas were layered in.

After the collapse, three gods stood up from the ruins. The Zeus type, hungry for power (gathering people through personal charisma and informal networks); the Athena type, where the capable take charge (forming temporary teams around problems); and the Dionysus type, who does not care about the organization at all (I am the independent expert; you come to me).

Handy's real insight is not that "organizations have four cultures." It is that the four are not parallel options — they rise and fall in succession. Apollo is the mainstream. Only after it falls do the other three gods get their chance. And Apollo will look reliable again the next time knowledge complexity rises, then fall again.

That cycle is now replaying in AI.

三、單體大模型就是那座塔

把這條敘事線套到今天。

2020 到 2023 年，AI 行業有一個幾乎沒人明說但人人都信的信念：造一個足夠大的模型，讓它包打天下。GPT-3 出來的時候，邏輯是這樣的——把全人類寫過的文本喂進去，模型就會獲得理解。這一次不一樣，這一次是「壓縮」。

它確實壓縮了。但壓縮到某個規模之後，問題開始集中爆發：

成本。 長上下文的推理開銷隨 token 數急劇上升，一個能讀二十萬字、能思考、能調工具的模型，每一次調用都是真金白銀。

注意力衰減。 上下文塞得越滿，模型對中段信息的召回越差。資料放在開頭和結尾還能用，放在中間基本就丟了。這不是 bug，是 Transformer 架構的固有屬性。

幻覺。 概率採樣的必然產物。你要求「不要編造」，它會盡力遵守，但沒有辦法保證。

確定性。 有些任務要求輸入 A 必須得到 B，誤差為零。概率系統給不了這個。

這四條壓在一起，單體路線的結論是清楚的：單一模型無法同時滿足成本、可解釋性、確定性和表達力的要求。 這不是模型不夠大，是架構類型不匹配。

而這恰好是阿波羅式結構的定義——一個角色，一套流程，撐起全部複雜性。當複雜性超過它的承載範圍，它不會優雅退化，它會塌方。

單體大模型就是那座塔。而它正在塌。

它塌得有多快

不誇張地說，工業界的反應速度快於大多數技術史上的一次架構轉向。

  • 2023 年開始，Function Calling 成為主流：模型不再直接輸出文本，而是輸出「我要調用這個函數，參數是這些」；
  • 2024 年，MCP（Model Context Protocol）發佈，將其定義為「連接 AI 助手與數據所在系統的新標準」，覆蓋內容庫、商業工具、開發環境三類目標。
  • 2026 年，多智能體系統（Multi-Agent）已經不是論文題目，是各家主力產品的默認架構。

從「一個大模型解決一切」到「一群專精節點各幹一段」，只用了三年。 漢迪當年寫這本書時，阿波羅塔塌了整整十年才被承認。

3. The Single Large Model Is That Tower

Apply that narrative line to today.

From 2020 to 2023, the AI industry held a belief almost no one stated aloud but everyone held: build one model big enough, and it will do everything. When GPT-3 arrived, the logic went like this — feed it all the text humanity has written, and the model will gain understanding. This time was different. This time it was "compression."

It did compress. But past a certain scale, problems began erupting together:

Cost. Inference cost for long contexts rises sharply with token count. A model that can read 200,000 words, think, and call tools costs real money on every single call.

Attention decay. The fuller the context, the worse the model's recall of middle-section information. Material at the beginning and the end still works. Material in the middle is mostly lost. This is not a bug. It is an inherent property of the Transformer architecture.

Hallucination. An inevitable product of probabilistic sampling. You tell it "do not fabricate," and it will try to comply. But there is no way to guarantee it.

Determinism. Some tasks require input A to produce B, with zero error. A probabilistic system cannot deliver that.

Press these four together, and the conclusion for the single-model route is clear: one model cannot simultaneously satisfy cost, explainability, determinism, and expressiveness. This is not about the model being too small. It is an architecture-type mismatch.

And this is exactly the definition of the Apollonian structure — one role, one set of procedures, holding up all complexity. When complexity exceeds its carrying capacity, it does not degrade gracefully. It collapses.

The single large model is that tower. And it is falling.

How Fast It Is Falling

Without exaggeration, the industry's reaction has been faster than most architecture shifts in the history of technology.

  • Starting in 2023, Function Calling went mainstream: instead of outputting text directly, the model outputs "I want to call this function, with these parameters";
  • In 2024, MCP (Model Context Protocol) was released, defined as "a new standard for connecting AI assistants to the systems where data lives," covering three target categories: content repositories, business tools, and development environments.
  • By 2026, multi-agent systems are no longer a paper topic. They are the default architecture of every major vendor's flagship products.

From "one big model solves everything" to "a group of specialized nodes, each doing one stretch," it took only three years. When Handy wrote his book, it took a full decade for Apollo's fall to be acknowledged.

我這套系統正好四神俱全，而且位置擺得挺標準：

  • 阿波羅是那些腳本和靜態斷言。它們不知道發消息的人是什麼，只知道「這條消息的格式必須符合這個格式」。它們是最不智能的部分，也是最不能省的部分。
  • 宙斯是Eva。它有全局視野，是唯一能讀完全部材料下判斷的角色。這是它價值最高的地方，也是它風險最大的地方。
  • 雅典娜是調用它的那個時刻——一有問題就把專家拉起來組成臨時班子，解決完解散。
  • 狄奧尼索斯是本地這一側。本地跑的、只碰本地文件、不上傳、不受中心調度。它是整套系統裡唯一可以「不聽宙斯」的組件。

五、Unix 剃刀：對「神格膨脹」最有效的解毒劑

講完分工，講一味藥。

1978 年，貝爾實驗室的 Doug McIlroy 等人在《貝爾系統技術雜誌》Unix Time-Sharing System 專號上總結了 Unix 哲學。 原文三條（這是原話，我逐條列，因為它們比任何轉述都鋒利）：

  1. Make each program do one thing well.（讓每個程序只做一件事，並做好它。）
  2. To do a new job, build afresh rather than complicate old programs by adding new 「features」.（要幹新活就重寫，別給老程序加「功能」。）
  3. Expect the output of every program to become the input of some other program.（要預期每個程序的輸出，成為另一個程序的輸入。） 第 4 條是後人總結：Salus 在 1994 年《A Quarter Century of UNIX》裡總結為「Write programs to handle text streams, because that is a universal interface.」（寫程序處理文本流，因為那是普適接口。）——這是總結，不是 1978 年原文。

第 3 條和 Salus 總結的第 4 條是這個系列的關鍵。Unix 的世界觀裡，程序不是角色，是管道的一段。 一個程序不「應該」有身份，它的價值完全在於它的輸出能不能被下一個程序消費。

這和漢迪的框架正好相反。漢迪講的是「誰在組織裡說了算」，Unix 講的是「東西怎麼拼起來」。

但兩者在實踐中經常是同一件事。 MCP 協議的本質上就是第 3 條加 Salus 第 4 條的網絡版：每個節點把自己的能力暴露成一個標準接口，輸出走統一格式，誰也不需要知道誰在後面。

剃刀的兩面

Unix 哲學裡還有一條後來被提煉為「奧卡姆剃刀」：如無必要，勿增實體。中文語境裡「奧卡姆」通常被當作一個獨立原則，但它在工程語境下更像是 Unix 第 2 條的哲學化版本——別為假設的未來加功能。

這條原則對 AI 架構的殺傷力最大。當前行業的典型症狀是：所有東西都要做成多智能體。 明明是三個字的字符串格式化，要拉三個 agent 開會討論；明明是查一行日誌，要走一遍「規劃—執行—反思」三段式。

多智能體是有代價的：token 消耗是數量級的差距，通信開銷隨節點數平方增長，跨節點一致性難以保證，迴音室風險隨討論輪次增加而上升。三個 agent 討論一件事的概率不會三倍好，它可能比一個好結果的概率更高，也可能更糟——取決於討論是否真的產生了新信息。

Unix 會說：先問這個問題需不需要多智能體。大概率不需要。 十幾行搜索命令配合一條斷言，比三個 agent 辯論一輪更可靠、更便宜、更快。

奧卡姆剃刀在這裡不是審美偏好，是成本紀律。 每一個「要不要引入一個 agent」的決定，都應該是默認「不」，需要論證才翻成「是」。

5. The Unix Razor: The Best Antidote to "Godhood Inflation"

Division of labor covered. Now a dose of medicine.

In 1978, Doug McIlroy and colleagues at Bell Labs summarized the Unix philosophy in a special issue of the Bell System Technical Journal on the Unix Time-Sharing System. The original three rules (I list them verbatim, because they are sharper than any paraphrase):

  1. Make each program do one thing well.
  2. To do a new job, build afresh rather than complicate old programs by adding new "features."
  3. Expect the output of every program to become the input of some other program. Rule 4 is a later summary: Salus summarized it in A Quarter Century of UNIX (1994) as "Write programs to handle text streams, because that is a universal interface." — a summary, not 1978 original text.

Rule 3 and Salus's summary rule 4 are the key to this series. In the Unix worldview, a program is not a role. It is a stretch of pipeline. A program is not "supposed" to have an identity. Its value lies entirely in whether its output can be consumed by the next program.

This is exactly the opposite of Handy's framework. Handy asks "who calls the shots in the organization." Unix asks "how do things fit together."

But in practice they are often the same thing. The essence of the MCP protocol is the network version of rule 3 plus Salus's rule 4: every node exposes its capability as a standard interface, output travels in a uniform format, and no one needs to know who is behind it.

The Two Sides of the Razor

The Unix philosophy also contains a rule later distilled as "Occam's razor": entities should not be multiplied beyond necessity. In Chinese-language contexts "Occam" is usually treated as a standalone principle, but in engineering contexts it looks more like a philosophical version of Unix rule 2 — do not add features for an assumed future.

This principle hits AI architecture hardest. The typical symptom in today's industry is: everything must be made multi-agent. A three-character string format calls for three agents to hold a meeting; a single log line lookup requires a full "plan — execute — reflect" three-stage process.

Multi-agent comes at a price: token consumption differs by orders of magnitude, communication overhead grows with the square of node count, cross-node consistency is hard to guarantee, and echo-chamber risk rises with each round of discussion. Three agents discussing one thing are not three times more likely to be right. They may be more likely than one good outcome, or they may be worse — it depends on whether the discussion actually produces new information.

Unix would say: first ask whether this problem needs multi-agent at all. Most likely it does not. A dozen lines of search commands plus one assertion is more reliable, cheaper, and faster than one round of debate among three agents.

Occam's razor here is not an aesthetic preference. It is cost discipline. Every decision of "should we bring in an agent" should default to "no," and flip to "yes" only with an argument.

六、反轉：這套系統越聰明，它的影子越長

到這裡，一個健康的技術敘事已經完成：單體塌了 → 聯邦建成了 → 紀律明確了。

但這個敘事有一個致命的前提：假設系統可以知道自己在做什麼。

而現在的系統不能。

這一節是全篇的轉折，也是最應該慢下來讀的一節。

哥德爾不完備：底座的天花板

1931 年，庫爾特·哥德爾證明瞭一個對所有形式系統都成立的定理：

任何足夠強大、能夠表達自己基礎算術的形式系統，如果它是一致的（不衝突），那它就不可能在系統內部證明自己是一致的。它必然存在至少一個真命題，但這個真命題在該系統內無法被證明。

這不只是數學結果。它的射程覆蓋整個邏輯系統的地基。

翻譯到 AI 系統： 一個多智能體系統裡，如果有一個 Agent 的職責是「檢查其他 Agent 的輸出是否正確」，那麼這個檢查器本身也在系統之內。它無法完整地檢查自己。 如果你再引入一個元檢查器來檢查它，那元檢查器同樣在系統內，同樣無法自證。這個鏈條必須在某處終止——而終止處，就是一個未經證明的假設。

任何規模的自指系統，最終都建立在一個它無法驗證的地基上。

這不是 bug。這是數學。

自指在工程裡的樣子：怪圈

道格拉斯·侯世達（Douglas Hofstadter）在《哥德爾、艾舍爾、巴赫》（GEB, 1979）裡把這件事講得更具體。他用「怪圈」（strange loop）這個概念：系統從某個層級跳出去，審視自己所在的層級，然後把這個跳出去的動作本身納入系統的定義。

他舉的例子是心智：神經元的突觸不知道自己在想什麼，但足夠多的突觸連成的網絡「知道」了。這種知道不需要在神經元層面被記錄。

免疫系統也是一個聯邦。各細胞只看一塊天，靠分辨「我」與「非我」不攻擊自己。怪圈是自體免疫：系統分不清自己與被評的對象，於是對自己開火。

在 AI 系統裡，怪圈有它的工程形態：自省循環。

現在的標配是 Reflexion 架構：執行者生成結果，批評者讀取結果並指出問題，執行者讀取批評並修改，再次生成。反覆，直到收斂。

這個架構有用，而且有用得驚人。但它同時是一個完美的自指陷阱。

問題一：檢查者與被檢查者共享地基。 批評者 Agent 和執行者 Agent 底層用同一套預訓練權重、同一批數據、同一個架構。執行者的偏見，批評者大概率也看不見——因為那是偏見本身的分佈形狀。批評者不是外部審查者，它是同一個人戴了副眼鏡。

上述自省架構的原始論文：Shinn, N., Cassano, F., Berman, E., Gopinath, A., Narasimhan, K., & Yao, S. 「Reflexion: Language Agents with Verbal Reinforcement Learning.」 arXiv:2303.11366, 2023.

問題二：多輪自省收斂到的可能不是真理，是共識。 三個 Agent 討論十輪，最後達成的「一致」可能只是一個共同幻覺。共識強度和正確性沒有因果關係，討論輪次增加只是讓所有人都更相信這個結論。

問題三：遞歸深度和深度成正比的危險。 反思層數越多，Token 成本呈指數上升，錯誤累積也同步放大。而且如果反思層自己出錯，那這個錯誤會被後續所有層當作「已修正的事實」。

侯世達會說：怪圈是心智的來源，也是心智的邊界。 你不能取消它——沒有自指就沒有自我認識。但你也不能信任它——它知道自己知道，不等於它知道。

我自己踩過的坑

這一節我不想只講理論。

我在自己的系統裡定了一條規矩：改一個活系統之後，不能靠「回讀自己剛寫的文件」來驗收，必須有跨重啟後的運行時證據。這條規矩是我在實際工作中被教訓出來的——不是從任何理論裡推出來的。

而這條規矩的本質，就是哥德爾定理的應用。 我改了一個腳本，然後讀一遍這個腳本，確認它看起來對。這個動作沒有任何價值，因為寫的人和讀的人是同一個信息來源。這不是驗證，這是自我循環。

這就是為什麼需要跨重啟。 跨重啟強制引入一個系統之外的事實——進程真的起來了、文件真的生成了、斷言真的報錯了。這是唯一一個能打破自指的機制。

在 AI 系統裡，我們沒有跨重啟這種奢侈。 一個模型改完自己的提示詞，然後評估自己的輸出——評估者和被評估者共享同一個上下文、同一批權重、同一次推理過程的所有前提。這是史上最完美的一個怪圈。

所以：所有「AI 自我改進」的說法，都要問一句——外部錨點在哪裡？

六、反轉：這套系統越聰明，它的影子越長

到這裡，一個健康的技術敘事已經完成：單體塌了 → 聯邦建成了 → 紀律明確了。

但這個敘事有一個致命的前提：假設系統可以知道自己在做什麼。

而現在的系統不能。

這一節是全篇的轉折，也是最應該慢下來讀的一節。

哥德爾不完備：底座的天花板

1931 年，庫爾特·哥德爾證明了一個對所有形式系統都成立的定理：

任何足夠強大、能夠表達自己基礎算術的形式系統，如果它是一致的（不衝突），那它就不可能在系統內部證明自己是一致的。它必然存在至少一個真命題，但這個真命題在該系統內無法被證明。

這不只是數學結果。它的射程覆蓋整個邏輯系統的地基。

翻譯到 AI 系統： 一個多智能體系統裡，如果有一個 Agent 的職責是「檢查其他 Agent 的輸出是否正確」，那麼這個檢查器本身也在系統之內。它無法完整地檢查自己。 如果你再引入一個元檢查器來檢查它，那元檢查器同樣在系統內，同樣無法自證。這個鏈條必須在某處終止——而終止處，就是一個未經證明的假設。

任何規模的自指系統，最終都建立在一個它無法驗證的地基上。

這不是 bug。這是數學。

自指在工程裡的樣子：怪圈

道格拉斯·侯世達（Douglas Hofstadter）在《哥德爾、艾舍爾、巴赫》（GEB, 1979）裡把這件事講得更具體。他用「怪圈」（strange loop）這個概念：系統從某個層級跳出去，審視自己所在的層級，然後把這個跳出去的動作本身納入系統的定義。

他舉的例子是心智：神經元的突觸不知道自己在想什麼，但足夠多的突觸連成的網絡「知道」了。這種知道不需要在神經元層面被記錄。

免疫系統也是一個聯邦。各細胞只看一塊天，靠分辨「我」與「非我」不攻擊自己。怪圈是自體免疫：系統分不清自己與被評的對象，於是對自己開火。

在 AI 系統裡，怪圈有它的工程形態：自省循環。

現在的標配是 Reflexion 架構：執行者生成結果，批評者讀取結果並指出問題，執行者讀取批評並修改，再次生成。反覆，直到收斂。

這個架構有用，而且有用得驚人。但它同時是一個完美的自指陷阱。

6. Reversal: The Smarter This System Gets, the Longer Its Shadow

Up to here, a healthy technical narrative is complete: the single model fell → the federation was built → the discipline is clear.

But this narrative has a fatal premise: it assumes the system can know what it is doing.

And today's systems cannot.

This section is the turn of the whole essay, and the one to read most slowly.

Gödel Incompleteness: The Ceiling of the Foundation

In 1931, Kurt Gödel proved a theorem that holds for all formal systems:

Any formal system strong enough to express its own basic arithmetic, if it is consistent (non-contradictory), cannot prove its own consistency from inside the system. There necessarily exists at least one true proposition that cannot be proven within that system.

This is not just a mathematical result. Its reach covers the foundation of the entire logical system.

Translated to AI systems: in a multi-agent system, if one agent's job is to "check whether other agents' outputs are correct," then that checker is itself inside the system. It cannot fully check itself. If you bring in a meta-checker to check it, that meta-checker is likewise inside the system, likewise unable to prove itself. This chain must terminate somewhere — and where it terminates is an unproven assumption.

Any self-referential system, at any scale, ultimately rests on a foundation it cannot verify.

This is not a bug. This is mathematics.

Self-Reference in Engineering: The Strange Loop

Douglas Hofstadter made this more concrete in Gödel, Escher, Bach (GEB, 1979). He used the concept of the "strange loop": a system jumps out of one level, examines the level it sits on, and then folds that very act of jumping out into the system's own definition.

His example is the mind: a neuron's synapse does not know what it is thinking, but a network of enough connected synapses "knows." That knowing does not need to be recorded at the neuron level.

The immune system is also a federation. Each cell sees only one patch of sky, and avoids attacking itself by telling "self" from "non-self." A strange loop is autoimmunity: the system cannot tell itself from the object it evaluates, so it opens fire on itself.

In AI systems, the strange loop has an engineering form: the self-reflection loop.

The current standard is the Reflexion architecture: the executor generates a result, the critic reads the result and points out problems, the executor reads the criticism and revises, then generates again. Repeat until convergence.

This architecture works, and works astonishingly well. But it is simultaneously a perfect self-reference trap.

Problem 1: checker and checked share a foundation. The critic agent and the executor agent run on the same pretrained weights, the same data, the same architecture. The executor's bias is very likely invisible to the critic too — because that is the shape of the bias itself. The critic is not an external reviewer. It is the same person wearing a pair of glasses.

Original paper for the self-reflection architecture described above: Shinn, N., Cassano, F., Berman, E., Gopinath, A., Narasimhan, K., & Yao, S. "Reflexion: Language Agents with Verbal Reinforcement Learning." arXiv:2303.11366, 2023.

Problem 2: multi-round self-reflection may converge not on truth, but on consensus. Three agents discussing for ten rounds may reach "agreement" that is only a shared hallucination. Consensus strength has no causal relationship with correctness. More rounds of discussion only make everyone believe the conclusion more.

Problem 3: the danger grows in proportion to recursion depth. The more reflection layers, the more token cost rises exponentially, and error accumulation amplifies in step. And if a reflection layer itself errs, that error gets taken by all subsequent layers as "already corrected fact."

Hofstadter would say: the strange loop is the source of mind, and also the boundary of mind. You cannot cancel it — without self-reference there is no self-knowledge. But you cannot trust it either — its knowing that it knows is not the same as its knowing.

A Pit I Stepped Into Myself

This section is not only about theory.

I set a rule in my own system: after changing a live system, you may not verify it by "rereading the file you just wrote." You must have across-restart runtime evidence. I learned this rule the hard way in real work — it was not derived from any theory.

And the essence of this rule is an application of Gödel's theorem. I changed a script, then read the script once, confirming it looked right. That action has no value, because the writer and the reader are the same information source. This is not verification. It is a self-loop.

That is why we need a cross-restart. A cross-restart forces in a fact from outside the system — the process really started, the file was really generated, the assertion really failed. It is the only mechanism that can break self-reference.

In AI systems, we do not have the luxury of a cross-restart. One model revises its own prompt, then evaluates its own output — evaluator and evaluated share the same context, the same weights, all the premises of the same inference run. This is the most perfect strange loop in history.

So: whenever anyone says "AI self-improvement," ask one question — where is the external anchor?

七、失控：這不是意外，這是代價

這一節會被壓得很短，但它不能刪。

凱文·凱利（Kevin Kelly）在《失控》（Out of Control, 1994）裡提出一個不太舒服的判斷：智能的湧現，必須以放棄控制權為代價。

蜂群能解決人類設計不出的問題，因為每一隻蜜蜂只遵循簡單局部規則，沒有任何一隻掌握全局。整體智能來自沒有中心。 反過來也成立：一旦你要求完全的控制、可預測的輸出、零意外，你就限制了系統能發現的解空間。

在多智能體系統裡，這是同一個取捨的工程版本：

追求 代價
中心編排（宙斯） 單點故障、上下文漂移、級聯崩潰
分佈式自治（雅典娜集群） 通信開銷、跨節點不一致、共識幻覺
嚴格規則（阿波羅護欄） 僵化、無法處理長尾、規則本身需要維護

沒有免費的一欄。每一欄都有代價，都必須明確選擇，而不是假裝它不存在。

大前研一在《無國界的世界》（The Borderless World, 1990）裡講的 4C 框架正在這個方向上被推得更遠：算力、模型、調用、數據正在變成可以跨境流動的貨幣和勞動力，邊界在協議層面而不是在物理層面。

但大前研一講的是資本和商品的穿透，我更在意的是這套網絡裡沒有公民。多智能體集群在協議層自由流動，但沒有身份、沒有責任、沒有邊界。它是一套極其高效的協作機制，也是一套沒有問責機制的系統。

這也是為什麼我這套系統的最後一道關是「人籤」 ——不是因為我信任模型，恰恰是因為我不信任任何系統能自己給自己定邊界。

7. Out of Control: Not an Accident, a Price

This section will be kept very short, but it cannot be cut.

Kevin Kelly put forward an uncomfortable judgment in Out of Control (1994): the emergence of intelligence must be paid for by giving up control.

A bee swarm can solve problems no human design could, because each bee follows only simple local rules, and no single bee holds the whole picture. Overall intelligence comes from having no center. The reverse also holds: once you demand complete control, predictable output, and zero surprises, you limit the solution space the system can discover.

In multi-agent systems, this is the engineering version of the same trade-off:

Pursuit Price
Central orchestration (Zeus) Single point of failure, context drift, cascading collapse
Distributed autonomy (Athena cluster) Communication overhead, cross-node inconsistency, consensus hallucination
Strict rules (Apollo guardrails) Rigidity, inability to handle the long tail, the rules themselves need maintenance

No column is free. Every column has a price, and it must be chosen explicitly, not pretended away.

Kenichi Ohmae's 4C framework in The Borderless World (1990) is being pushed further in this direction: compute, models, invocation, and data are becoming currency and labor that flow across borders, with boundaries at the protocol layer rather than the physical layer.

But Ohmae writes about the penetration of capital and goods. What concerns me more is that there are no citizens in this network. Multi-agent clusters flow freely at the protocol layer, but with no identity, no responsibility, no boundary. It is an extremely efficient collaboration mechanism, and also a system with no accountability mechanism.

That is also why the last gate in my system is a "human sign-off" — not because I trust models, but precisely because I do not trust any system to set its own boundary.

八、結論：諸神聯邦沒有牧師

漢迪講的故事裡，神是多元的，組織是它們的角鬥場。 每一尊神都有自己的轄區、自己的追隨者、自己的行事邏輯。

多智能體系統照搬了這個結構。 但有一個關鍵差別。

漢迪的神話裡沒有工程師。組織是自然長出來的，力量此消彼長，沒有人設計誰服從誰。

而多智能體系統是有人設計的。 每個 Agent 的職責、邊界、權限、通信協議都是人寫死的。那四張映射表裡每一行都對應一行代碼。

這意味著：諸神聯邦不是信仰的結果，是工程選擇的結果。 它可以被設計、被調整、被關掉某個節點。

這既是它的力量，也是它的限度。 我們可以在系統裡選擇「誰聽誰的」——我這套系統選擇了一個跑腿的腳本擁有最終否決權，這是反直覺的，但它是對的，因為它是那個成本最低、誤判代價最小的組件。

漢迪的塔塌是歷史。 你的塔塌不塌，什麼時候塌，塌了之後用哪套新結構——這是工程問題。

不把它交給神話。

8. Conclusion: The Federation of Gods Has No Priest

In Handy's story, the gods are plural, and organizations are their arena. Each god has its own territory, its own followers, its own way of doing things.

Multi-agent systems copied this structure. But with one critical difference.

In Handy's mythology there is no engineer. Organizations grow naturally. Powers wax and wane. No one designs who obeys whom.

Multi-agent systems are designed by someone. Every agent's duties, boundaries, permissions, and communication protocols are written down by a human. Every row in those four mapping tables corresponds to a line of code.

That means: the federation of gods is not the result of belief. It is the result of an engineering choice. It can be designed, adjusted, and switched off node by node.

That is both its power and its limit. We can choose in the system "who listens to whom" — my system chose to give an errand-running script the final veto. That is counterintuitive, but it is right, because it is the component with the lowest cost and the smallest price for a misjudgment.

Handy's tower fell as history. Whether your tower falls, when it falls, and which new structure you use after it falls — that is an engineering question.

Do not hand it over to myth.

James P. Carse 在《有限與無限的遊戲》（Free Press, 1986）裡區分：有限遊戲是為贏而玩，無限遊戲是為繼續玩而玩；兩者的規則功能相反——有限遊戲的規則用來保證遊戲結束，無限遊戲的規則用來保證它不結束。

按這個框架，把一個單體模型拆成一個多智能體聯邦，是一個典型的有限遊戲動作。

理由很直接：單體模型在能力上有一個理論上限，而這個上限是不可預先窮盡的——它屬於無限遊戲的形狀。而聯邦的整個意義，是給這個沒有終點的對象劃出一個可管理的邊界：把一個攻不下來的問題，拆成若干個各自可驗收的子任務。這不是把無限遊戲變得更好玩了，是給它安了一個終點。

所以本篇第三節那個結論需要補一句：哥德爾怪圈不是聯邦的缺陷，是聯邦這個裝置的證明。 聯邦之所以需要「各神只看得見自己那塊天」這樣的結構，正是因為完整視角在系統內不可得。而一旦你把各神的天拼起來想補全，你會得到第五篇說的那個東西——一份要求你自己寫不出的規則。

而「諸神」這個命名，在這個框架下也換了位置。 第一篇我說神名是減震器和劃界；本篇我說聯邦是工程選擇。第五篇把這兩句合起來：用一個神的名字，去稱呼一個為了贏而設計的分工結構——這個命名同時掩蓋了它的兩重身份（有效的工程裝置 / 一個已經結束了的賭局）。

Unix 那四條原則在這裡也說得通了。我前面把它們當作「少做多做的銳利」，但它們更準確的性質是：這是一套讓系統可以無限延續下去、而不需要任何人宣佈它完成的規則。 過濾器留下要的東西丟掉不要的東西、失敗靜默退出、小工具串成大工具——每一條都不產生一個「完成」的信號。

而這一點，是本篇當時沒有看見的：Unix 哲學其實站在無限遊戲那一邊。 我用它的銳利去論證有限遊戲的聯邦，這中間有一道我沒有跨過去的裂縫。 它之所以看起來那麼適合多智能體系統，正因為它壓根不關心勝負——它關心的是這件事明天還能不能繼續。

塔塌了，聯邦搭起來了——可誰來保證，它明天還能繼續？

——白石溪

Appendix: The Federation Is a Finite-Game Tactic

This essay's conclusion stops at "this is an engineering choice." But looking back from essay five, this engineering choice will turn out to have a more precise name.

James P. Carse distinguishes in Finite and Infinite Games (Free Press, 1986): finite games are played to win; infinite games are played to keep playing. The functions of their rules are opposite — the rules of a finite game exist to guarantee the game ends; the rules of an infinite game exist to guarantee it does not end.

Under this framework, splitting a single model into a multi-agent federation is a typical finite-game move.

The reason is direct: a single model has a theoretical capability ceiling, and that ceiling cannot be exhaustively mapped in advance — it has the shape of an infinite game. And the entire point of the federation is to draw a manageable boundary around an object with no endpoint: break a problem that cannot be taken down whole into sub-tasks that can each be verified. This does not make the infinite game more fun to play. It installs an endpoint in it.

So the conclusion in Section 3 of this essay needs one more sentence: the Gödel strange loop is not a defect of the federation. It is proof of the federation as a device. The reason the federation needs a structure where "each god sees only its own patch of sky" is precisely that a complete view is unattainable inside the system. And once you try to stitch the gods' skies together to complete the picture, you get what essay five describes — a rule you cannot write yourself.

And the name "gods," under this framework, also changes place. In essay one I said the god name is a shock absorber and a boundary; in this essay I said the federation is an engineering choice. Essay five puts the two sentences together: to call a division-of-labor structure designed for winning by a god's name — this naming simultaneously hides its two identities (an effective engineering device / a gamble that has already ended).

The four Unix principles make sense here too. Earlier I treated them as "the sharpness of doing less and doing more," but their more precise nature is: a set of rules that lets a system go on indefinitely without anyone having to declare it finished. Filters keep what is wanted and discard what is not, failures exit silently, small tools chain into big tools — not one of them produces a "done" signal.

And this is the point this essay did not see at the time: Unix philosophy actually stands on the infinite-game side. I used its sharpness to argue for a finite-game federation. There is a crack in between that I did not cross. The reason it looks so fitting for multi-agent systems is precisely that it does not care about winning or losing at all — it cares whether this thing can still go on tomorrow.

The tower fell, the federation is built — but who guarantees it will still go on tomorrow?

— WhiteStoneXi
