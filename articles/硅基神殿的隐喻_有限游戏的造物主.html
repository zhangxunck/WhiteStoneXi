第五篇：有限游戏的造物主

主线理论：James P. Carse《有限与无限的游戏》(1986)

中文欄：預設繁體，按右側切換簡體 · 右欄 English

一、一個無法收尾的行業

先說一個結構上的怪事。

2015 年之前，行業裡衡量一個模型的尺子很樸素：它答對多少題。 今天，尺子變成了：某個基準上的分數。然後那個分數被刷滿，於是出現新的基準。然後新的基準也被刷滿。

這個循環沒有終點。 它不是某個公司沒做好，導致大家換了個賽道；而是這個循環在結構上無法結束——因為每產生一個"最好的成績"，就同時產生一個"標準已達成"的事實，而"標準已達成"是有限的定義。

於是我們得到一個奇怪的組合：

技術本身是無限遊戲的形狀。 模型沒有一個"完成"狀態。你無法在訓練開始時寫出"它會做什麼"的完整規格，然後照著驗收。能力是在訓練過程中長出來的，不是被安裝上去的——這就是"湧現"這個詞原本的意思。你也贏不了它。基準分數被追平不等於任何東西被完成，因為一個追平了的基準立刻變成一個過期的尺子。

但我們組織這門技術的方式，是有限遊戲的形狀。 排行榜、市場份額、參數量、發佈會日期、估值——每一個都是有限遊戲的計數器，因為它們都有"第一名"這個位置，而一個只有第一名、沒有終點的排行榜，本身是矛盾的。

我把這個錯位稱為用無限遊戲的技術，打有限遊戲的仗。

這不是道德指控。它是一個結構描述。而結構描述的好處是：它可以被追問"錯在哪"，道德指控不行。

一、一个无法收尾的行业

先说一个结构上的怪事。

2015 年之前，行业里衡量一个模型的尺子很朴素：它答对多少题。 今天，尺子变成了：某个基准上的分数。然后那个分数被刷满，于是出现新的基准。然后新的基准也被刷满。

这个循环没有终点。 它不是某个公司没做好，导致大家换了个赛道；而是这个循环在结构上无法结束——因为每产生一个"最好的成绩"，就同时产生一个"标准已达成"的事实，而"标准已达成"是有限的定义。

于是我们得到一个奇怪的组合：

技术本身是无限游戏的形状。 模型没有一个"完成"状态。你无法在训练开始时写出"它会做什么"的完整规格，然后照着验收。能力是在训练过程中长出来的，不是被安装上去的——这就是"涌现"这个词原本的意思。你也赢不了它。基准分数被追平不等于任何东西被完成，因为一个追平了的基准立刻变成一个过期的尺子。

但我们组织这门技术的方式，是有限游戏的形状。 排行榜、市场份额、参数量、发布会日期、估值——每一个都是有限游戏的计数器，因为它们都有"第一名"这个位置，而一个只有第一名、没有终点的排行榜，本身是矛盾的。

我把这个错位称为用无限游戏的技术，打有限游戏的仗。

这不是道德指控。它是一个结构描述。而结构描述的好处是：它可以被追问"错在哪"，道德指控不行。

I. An Industry That Cannot Be Closed Out

Let me start with a structural oddity.

Before 2015, the industry's yardstick for measuring a model was plain: how many questions it answered correctly. Today the yardstick is a score on some benchmark. Then that score is maxed out, and a new benchmark appears. Then the new benchmark is maxed out too.

This loop has no end. It is not that some company failed and everyone switched tracks; the loop is structurally incapable of ending—because each time a "best result" is produced, a fact of "standard met" is produced at the same time, and "standard met" is a finite definition.

So we end up with a strange combination:

The technology itself has the shape of an infinite game. A model has no "completed" state. You cannot write out at the start of training a full specification of "what it will do" and then accept it against that. Capabilities grow out of the training process; they are not installed—this is what "emergence" originally meant. Nor can you beat it. A benchmark being matched does not mean anything has been completed, because a matched benchmark instantly becomes an expired yardstick.

But the way we organize this technology has the shape of a finite game. Leaderboards, market share, parameter counts, launch dates, valuations—each is a counter of the finite game, because each has a "number one" position, and a leaderboard that has a number one but no finish line is itself a contradiction.

I call this mismatch fighting the finite-game war with infinite-game technology.

This is not a moral accusation. It is a structural description. And the advantage of a structural description is that it can be pressed with "where exactly is it wrong"; a moral accusation cannot.

二、Carse 的區分

1986 年，一位在紐約大學教宗教的教授出了一本 150 頁的小書。James P. Carse，《有限與無限的遊戲：作為遊戲與可能性的生命願景》（Finite and Infinite Games: A Vision of Life as Play and Possibility，New York: The Free Press, 1986）。

這本書第一章第一句是：

"There are at least two kinds of games. One could be called finite, the other infinite. A finite game is played for the purpose of winning, an infinite game for the purpose of continuing the play." （至少有兩種遊戲。一種可以叫做有限的，另一種可以叫做無限的。有限遊戲是為贏而玩，無限遊戲是為繼續玩而玩。）

這一句就夠用了。 但我想強調的是它經常被讀錯的地方。

Carse 的書被引用時通常被當成一本勵志讀物："要玩無限遊戲，要眼光長遠，不要執著於輸贏。"這個讀法把一本書裡最鋒利的東西磨平了。 因為 Carse 的區分不是道德訓誡，是兩個結構上不同的數學對象——他自己在扉頁說明寫這本書不需要高等數學背景（Without advanced mathematical skills），但它講的確實是形式。

那麼兩者的結構差別到底在哪？出版社的官方摘要給了最乾淨的一份（WorldCat 收錄，出版方提供）：

"Finite games are played in order to be won, which is when they end. [...] Infinite games [...] Their object is not winning, but ensuring the continuation of play. The rules may change, the boundaries may change, even the participants may change—as long as the game is never allowed to come to an end." （有限遊戲為贏而玩，贏的時候遊戲就結束。無限遊戲的對象不是贏，而是確保遊戲繼續。規則可以變，邊界可以變，參與者甚至可以變——只要遊戲不被允許結束。）

請注意這句話的力學方向。 有限遊戲的規則是用來保證遊戲會結束的；無限遊戲的規則是用來保證遊戲不結束的。這是完全相反的兩種規則功能，不是同一套規則的兩種態度。

還有兩句更要緊的，我核過原書頁碼：有限遊戲的玩家在邊界之內玩，無限遊戲的玩家與邊界一起玩（"Finite players play within boundaries; infinite players play with boundaries."，1986 年初版 p.10，第十一節末）；以及——

"if surprise is no longer possible, all play ceases." （如果不再可能有意外，所有的遊戲都停止。1986 年初版 p.18，第十七至十八節。）

前一句的意思是：有限遊戲把邊界當裁判給的界線，無限遊戲把邊界當自己手裡的材料。 規則不再是被遵守的東西，而是被重新設計的對象。這兩句是理解整篇的關鍵，所以我在原書裡逐字核過，頁碼是 1986 年初版的印次頁碼。

最後一層。 這本書的結尾是：

"There is but one infinite game." （只有一個無限遊戲。1986 年初版 p.149，第一百零一節——這是全書的正文末句。）

這裡要停一下，因為大部分人引到這就把話說完，其實卡瑟沒有說完。

廣泛流傳的完整句是 "There is but one infinite game. And we are to understand that the only infinite game is life itself."（只有一個無限遊戲，而那唯一的無限遊戲就是生命本身）。但我把 1986 年初版全書檢索了一遍：卡瑟寫完 "There is but one infinite game." 就結束了這一段，"the only infinite game is life itself" 這個後半句全書沒有出現過。"life itself" 一詞全書只出現一次，是他在前面引馬克思，與無限遊戲無關。

後半句是讀者接上去的。我不打算把它當作卡瑟的原話——因為那句話把一件不可選擇的事，說成了一句可以被引用的話。

卡瑟實際寫下的更剋制，也更有意思：他只宣佈了那個遊戲的唯一性，沒有說那個遊戲是什麼。 有限遊戲是可選的——你可以不下棋。而那個唯一的無限遊戲不是選項，它是那個在你不下任何棋的時候仍然在繼續的東西。它不需要被命名才開始。

而這就引出了 AI 時代最具體的一個問題。 如果唯一的無限遊戲是生命本身，而生命的具體形態是"活著、知道自己在活著、然後繼續"——那麼一門以"生成文本"為目標的技術，它在結構上能接觸到生命這一層嗎？

我在這一篇裡的答案是：它能生成那個形狀，但形狀不是那個東西。 而我不敢說這句話是錯的。

二、Carse 的区分

1986 年，一位在纽约大学教宗教的教授出了一本 150 页的小书。James P. Carse，《有限与无限的游戏：作为游戏与可能性的生命愿景》（Finite and Infinite Games: A Vision of Life as Play and Possibility，New York: The Free Press, 1986）。

这本书第一章第一句是：

"There are at least two kinds of games. One could be called finite, the other infinite. A finite game is played for the purpose of winning, an infinite game for the purpose of continuing the play." （至少有两种游戏。一种可以叫做有限的，另一种可以叫做无限的。有限游戏是为赢而玩，无限游戏是为继续玩而玩。）

这一句就够用了。 但我想强调的是它经常被读错的地方。

Carse 的书被引用时通常被当成一本励志读物："要玩无限游戏，要眼光长远，不要执着于输赢。"这个读法把一本书里最锋利的东西磨平了。 因为 Carse 的区分不是道德训诫，是两个结构上不同的数学对象——他自己在扉页说明写这本书不需要高等数学背景（Without advanced mathematical skills），但它讲的确实是形式。

那么两者的结构差别到底在哪？出版社的官方摘要给了最干净的一份（WorldCat 收录，出版方提供）：

"Finite games are played in order to be won, which is when they end. [...] Infinite games [...] Their object is not winning, but ensuring the continuation of play. The rules may change, the boundaries may change, even the participants may change—as long as the game is never allowed to come to an end." （有限游戏为赢而玩，赢的时候游戏就结束。无限游戏的对象不是赢，而是确保游戏继续。规则可以变，边界可以变，参与者甚至可以变——只要游戏不被允许结束。）

请注意这句话的力学方向。 有限游戏的规则是用来保证游戏会结束的；无限游戏的规则是用来保证游戏不结束的。这是完全相反的两种规则功能，不是同一套规则的两种态度。

还有两句更要紧的，我核过原书页码：有限游戏的玩家在边界之内玩，无限游戏的玩家与边界一起玩（"Finite players play within boundaries; infinite players play with boundaries."，1986 年初版 p.10，第十一节末）；以及——

"if surprise is no longer possible, all play ceases." （如果不再可能有意外，所有的游戏都停止。1986 年初版 p.18，第十七至十八节。）

前一句的意思是：有限游戏把边界当裁判给的界线，无限游戏把边界当自己手里的材料。 规则不再是被遵守的东西，而是被重新设计的对象。这两句是理解整篇的关键，所以我在原书里逐字核过，页码是 1986 年初版的印次页码。

最后一层。 这本书的结尾是：

"There is but one infinite game." （只有一个无限游戏。1986 年初版 p.149，第一百零一节——这是全书的正文末句。）

这里要停一下，因为大部分人引到这就把话说完，其实卡瑟没有说完。

广泛流传的完整句是 "There is but one infinite game. And we are to understand that the only infinite game is life itself."（只有一个无限游戏，而那唯一的无限游戏就是生命本身）。但我把 1986 年初版全书检索了一遍：卡瑟写完 "There is but one infinite game." 就结束了这一段，"the only infinite game is life itself" 这个后半句全书没有出现过。"life itself" 一词全书只出现一次，是他在前面引马克思，与无限游戏无关。

后半句是读者接上去的。我不打算把它当作卡瑟的原话——因为那句话把一件不可选择的事，说成了一句可以被引用的话。

卡瑟实际写下的更克制，也更有意思：他只宣布了那个游戏的唯一性，没有说那个游戏是什么。 有限游戏是可选的——你可以不下棋。而那个唯一的无限游戏不是选项，它是那个在你不下任何棋的时候仍然在继续的东西。它不需要被命名才开始。

而这就引出了 AI 时代最具体的一个问题。 如果唯一的无限游戏是生命本身，而生命的具体形态是"活着、知道自己在活着、然后继续"——那么一门以"生成文本"为目标的技术，它在结构上能接触到生命这一层吗？

我在这一篇里的答案是：它能生成那个形状，但形状不是那个东西。 而我不敢说这句话是错的。

II. Carse's Distinction

In 1986, a professor of religion at New York University published a small 150-page book: James P. Carse, Finite and Infinite Games: A Vision of Life as Play and Possibility (New York: The Free Press, 1986).

The first sentence of its first chapter is:

"There are at least two kinds of games. One could be called finite, the other infinite. A finite game is played for the purpose of winning, an infinite game for the purpose of continuing the play."

This one sentence is enough. But I want to stress where it is most often misread.

When Carse is cited, the book is usually treated as self-help: "play the infinite game, take the long view, don't cling to winning or losing." This reading blunts the sharpest thing in the book. Carse's distinction is not a moral exhortation; it is about two structurally different formal objects—he notes on the title page that the book needs no advanced mathematics (Without advanced mathematical skills), and yet what it deals with is indeed form.

So where is the structural difference? The publisher's blurb gives the cleanest statement (as catalogued by WorldCat, publisher-supplied):

"Finite games are played in order to be won, which is when they end. [...] Infinite games [...] Their object is not winning, but ensuring the continuation of play. The rules may change, the boundaries may change, even the participants may change—as long as the game is never allowed to come to an end."

Note the direction of the force. A finite game's rules exist to guarantee that the game will end; an infinite game's rules exist to guarantee that the game will not end. These are two opposite functions of rules, not two attitudes toward the same rules.

Two more lines, even more to the point, which I checked against the original's pagination: players of the finite game play within the boundaries, players of the infinite game play with the boundaries ("Finite players play within boundaries; infinite players play with boundaries," 1986 first edition p.10, end of section 11); and—

"if surprise is no longer possible, all play ceases." (1986 first edition p.18, sections 17–18.)

The first means: the finite game treats the boundary as a line drawn by the referee; the infinite game treats the boundary as material in one's own hands. The rules are no longer something obeyed; they are something redesigned. These two lines are the key to the whole essay, so I checked them word for word; the page numbers are those of the 1986 first edition.

The final layer. The book ends with:

"There is but one infinite game." (1986 first edition p.149, section 101—the last sentence of the book's main text.)

Stop here, because most who quote this think the statement is finished, when Carse was not finished.

The sentence that circulates widely as the complete one is "There is but one infinite game. And we are to understand that the only infinite game is life itself." But I searched the entire 1986 first edition: after "There is but one infinite game." Carse ended the passage there; the second half, "the only infinite game is life itself," never appears in the book. The phrase "life itself" occurs once in the whole book, in an earlier quotation of Marx, and has nothing to do with the infinite game.

That second half was supplied by readers. I will not treat it as Carse's own words—because that sentence turns something that cannot be chosen into something that can be quoted.

What Carse actually wrote is more restrained, and more interesting: he announced only that the game is unique; he did not say what it is. Finite games are optional—you can choose not to play chess. The one infinite game is not an option; it is the thing that keeps going even when you are not playing any game at all. It does not need to be named before it begins.

And this leads to the most concrete question of the AI era. If the one infinite game is life itself, and the concrete form of life is "being alive, knowing that one is alive, and continuing"—can a technology whose goal is "generating text" reach that layer of life at the structural level?

My answer here is: it can generate that shape, but the shape is not the thing. And I am not in a position to say this claim is wrong.

三、AI 是無限遊戲的技術：三個證據

先把"為什麼說它是無限遊戲形狀"講實。三個證據，都不需要哲學。

證據一：能力不可預先完全指定。 一個被完整設計好的系統，其行為是可從規格推出的。而大模型的相當一部分能力（包括"會寫代碼""會解釋概念"這類看起來最該被設計的）是在訓練過程中出現的，不在任何一份規格文檔裡。這在形式上就是"規則不能被提前寫完"——而規則不能被提前寫完，正是無限遊戲的定義條件之一。

證據二：沒有終止狀態。 訓練可以被叫停（這是工程決定，不是邏輯必然），但停下來的模型沒有"完成了"這個屬性。一個可被叫停的東西和一個有終點的遊戲，不是同一個類型。

證據三：飽和是必然的，而且飽和之後繼續。 一個基準被刷滿之後，它不是"解決了這個問題"，是"這把尺子到期了"。於是必須換一把尺子。這是一個自我延續的循環，而它沒有自然終點——和 Carse 說的"如果不再可能有意外，遊戲就停止"正好相反：這裡的新意外不是來自人，是來自尺子的失效。

注意這三個證據都在講形式，不講意義。 我沒有說"模型在進行無限遊戲"。我說的是：這套技術被生產出來的方式，無法被一個有限遊戲的驗收標準窮盡。 這兩句差得很遠，後面第六節要處理這個差別帶來的全部麻煩。

三、AI 是无限游戏的技术：三个证据

先把"为什么说它是无限游戏形状"讲实。三个证据，都不需要哲学。

证据一：能力不可预先完全指定。 一个被完整设计好的系统，其行为是可从规格推出的。而大模型的相当一部分能力（包括"会写代码""会解释概念"这类看起来最该被设计的）是在训练过程中出现的，不在任何一份规格文档里。这在形式上就是"规则不能被提前写完"——而规则不能被提前写完，正是无限游戏的定义条件之一。

证据二：没有终止状态。 训练可以被叫停（这是工程决定，不是逻辑必然），但停下来的模型没有"完成了"这个属性。一个可被叫停的东西和一个有终点的游戏，不是同一个类型。

证据三：饱和是必然的，而且饱和之后继续。 一个基准被刷满之后，它不是"解决了这个问题"，是"这把尺子到期了"。于是必须换一把尺子。这是一个自我延续的循环，而它没有自然终点——和 Carse 说的"如果不再可能有意外，游戏就停止"正好相反：这里的新意外不是来自人，是来自尺子的失效。

注意这三个证据都在讲形式，不讲意义。 我没有说"模型在进行无限游戏"。我说的是：这套技术被生产出来的方式，无法被一个有限游戏的验收标准穷尽。 这两句差得很远，后面第六节要处理这个差别带来的全部麻烦。

III. AI Is the Technology of the Infinite Game: Three Pieces of Evidence

First let me make solid the claim "why it has the shape of the infinite game." Three pieces of evidence, none of them requiring philosophy.

Evidence one: capabilities cannot be fully specified in advance. A fully designed system has behavior derivable from its specification. But much of a large model's capability—including what looks most designable, like "writing code" and "explaining concepts"—appears during training and is in no specification document. Formally this is "the rules cannot be written in advance"—and rules not writable in advance is one of the defining conditions of the infinite game.

Evidence two: there is no terminal state. Training can be stopped (an engineering decision, not a logical necessity), but a stopped model has no property called "finished." Something stoppable and a game with a finish line are not the same kind of thing.

Evidence three: saturation is inevitable, and the play continues after it. Once a benchmark is maxed out, that is not "this problem is solved" but "this yardstick has expired." So a new yardstick must be used. This is a self-perpetuating loop with no natural end—the opposite of Carse's "if surprise is no longer possible, play ceases": here the new surprises come not from people but from the failure of the yardstick.

Note that all three concern form, not meaning. I have not said "the model is playing an infinite game." What I have said is: the way this technology comes into being cannot be exhausted by any finite game's acceptance criteria. Those two statements are far apart, and section six must deal with all the trouble that gap creates.

四、同一套技術，有限遊戲式地部署

上面三條都是關於技術的。接下來的三條是關於我們的。兩者之間的落差，就是這一篇的全部內容。

證據一：所有公開指標都是有限遊戲的指標。 參數量、基準分、排行榜名次、市佔率、定價檔位。注意每一項都隱含一個"第一名"的位置，而一個只有第一名、沒有終點的排行榜，在形式上是自相矛盾的。

證據二：對齊技術是一種人為設定的終止條件。 RLHF 這類方法，用一句技術描述就夠了：它用一個人類偏好作為優化終點。這在形式上，是把"讓某個指標最大化"定義成了"遊戲結束"。 而偏好是有偏的、有時間性的、並且不是它自己產生的。

這一點我認為需要精確一點，因為很容易被說成一句廉價話。 偏好不等於錯誤。把"人類覺得好"當終點，其正當性在於：人類是使用結果的承受者，讓承受者的偏好參與優化，在原則上完全合理。

問題不在於不該有這個終點。問題在於：它是唯一一個被合法承認的終點。 當一個被行業公認合法的終止條件存在時，其他"這件事還可以繼續"的判斷，就自動變成"還沒做完"——而"還沒做完"是可以被催促的。

證據三：敘事層面的時間感是有限的。 "我們正在進入 AGI""下一代模型將會……""這是最後一代需要人類監督的模型"——每一句都設定了完成態。 無限遊戲的一個基本特徵是它不承諾完成；而關於 AI 的主流敘事，幾乎每一句都在承諾完成。

四、同一套技术，有限游戏式地部署

上面三条都是关于技术的。接下来的三条是关于我们的。两者之间的落差，就是这一篇的全部内容。

证据一：所有公开指标都是有限游戏的指标。 参数量、基准分、排行榜名次、市占率、定价档位。注意每一项都隐含一个"第一名"的位置，而一个只有第一名、没有终点的排行榜，在形式上是自相矛盾的。

证据二：对齐技术是一种人为设定的终止条件。 RLHF 这类方法，用一句技术描述就够了：它用一个人类偏好作为优化终点。这在形式上，是把"让某个指标最大化"定义成了"游戏结束"。 而偏好是有偏的、有时间性的、并且不是它自己产生的。

这一点我认为需要精确一点，因为很容易被说成一句廉价话。 偏好不等于错误。把"人类觉得好"当终点，其正当性在于：人类是使用结果的承受者，让承受者的偏好参与优化，在原则上完全合理。

问题不在于不该有这个终点。问题在于：它是唯一一个被合法承认的终点。 当一个被行业公认合法的终止条件存在时，其他"这件事还可以继续"的判断，就自动变成"还没做完"——而"还没做完"是可以被催促的。

证据三：叙事层面的时间感是有限的。 "我们正在进入 AGI""下一代模型将会……""这是最后一代需要人类监督的模型"——每一句都设定了完成态。 无限游戏的一个基本特征是它不承诺完成；而关于 AI 的主流叙事，几乎每一句都在承诺完成。

IV. The Same Technology, Deployed as a Finite Game

The three points above are about the technology. The next three are about us. The gap between the two is the entire content of this essay.

Evidence one: every public metric is a finite-game metric. Parameter counts, benchmark scores, leaderboard rank, market share, pricing tiers. Note that each one implies a "number one" position, and a leaderboard that has a number one but no finish line is formally self-contradictory.

Evidence two: alignment is an artificially set termination condition. Methods such as RLHF can be described in one technical sentence: they use human preference as the optimization endpoint. Formally, this defines "maximize some metric" as "the game ends." And preferences are biased, time-bound, and not self-generated.

I think this point needs to be stated precisely, because it is easy to say cheaply. Preference is not the same as error. Taking "what humans feel is good" as the endpoint is defensible, because: humans are the bearers of the consequences, and letting the bearers' preferences enter the optimization is, in principle, perfectly reasonable.

The problem is not that this endpoint should not exist. The problem is: it is the only endpoint that is legally acknowledged. When an industry-wide, legitimate termination condition exists, any other judgment that "this can still go on" automatically becomes "not yet done"—and "not yet done" can be urged along.

Evidence three: the narrative sense of time is finite. "We are about to enter AGI." "The next generation of models will…" "This is the last generation of models that needs human oversight."—each sentence sets a completion state. A basic feature of the infinite game is that it does not promise completion; yet nearly every sentence of mainstream AI narrative promises completion.

五、兩種終局

有限遊戲有兩種死法，而它們長得完全不一樣。這是赫胥黎和奧威爾的差別，也是這一篇最實際的一個區分。

赫胥黎：讓所有人主動退出

1932 年的《美麗新世界》裡，"soma" 不是讓人不痛苦，是讓人不再需要痛苦這個能力。關於它的機制，有這樣一段（第十七章，穆斯塔法·蒙德對約翰說）：

"And there's always soma to calm your anger, to reconcile you to your enemies, to make you patient and long-suffering. [...] Anybody can be virtuous now. You can carry at least half of your morality about in a bottle. Christianity without tears—that's what soma is."

"你可以在瓶子裡裝著你道德的一半。" 這句話的分量在於：它許諾的不是舒適，是可攜帶。而任何被優化成可攜帶的東西，都已經脫離了產生它的條件。

列寧娜對伯納德說這段更直接，小說 p.91：

"Why don't you take soma when you have these dreadful ideas of yours. You'd forget all about them. And instead of being miserable, you'd be jolly." （你有這些可怕的想法時，為什麼不吃點 soma 呢？你會把它們全忘掉。然後你就不痛苦了，會很快樂。）

注意她給的理由：不是因為它對，是因為它會讓你不再不舒服。

這就是有限遊戲的理想終局：沒有人輸，沒有人贏，遊戲在所有人都不再想玩的時候結束。 而它不需要暴力——小說裡林達是被 soma 反覆使用耗死的，但這不是"被敵人殺死"，是"被自己選擇的舒適消耗掉"。

今天的技術敘事大量是這種形狀的。 它承諾的不是你變強，是你不必再面對那些讓你難受的東西。便利、自動化、"不用再自己判斷了"——每一條都在降低你需要進入遊戲的可能性。

奧威爾：永遠不結束，也永遠不前進

1949 年的《一九八四》裡，Big Brother 作用於身體，思想警察作用於行為，而新話作用於語言本身。三重思維的定義（第一部第三章）是：

"The power of holding two contradictory beliefs in one's mind simultaneously, and accepting both of them." （同時在腦中持有兩種矛盾信念，並且同時接受兩者的力量。）

奧威爾自己給出的三個互斥項是：戰爭即和平、自由即奴役、無知即力量。

關鍵在定義裡的那個限定詞：held in one's mind。 雙重思維不是"被強迫相信謊言"——那是有痛苦有抵抗的。雙重思維是同時持有互斥的表述，而它們不被允許相遇。兩個命題都在場，但從不併置在一個焦點上。

這在形式上正是"保證遊戲不結束的規則"。 奧威爾的小說裡，黨的口號自相矛盾，而黨在形式上是不可擊敗的——因為任何一次真正的對質都會讓矛盾被同時看見，而那正是黨要避免的事。

五、两种终局

有限游戏有两种死法，而它们长得完全不一样。这是赫胥黎和奥威尔的差别，也是这一篇最实际的一个区分。

赫胥黎：让所有人主动退出

"And there's always soma to calm your anger, to reconcile you to your enemies, to make you patient and long-suffering. [...] Anybody can be virtuous now. You can carry at least half of your morality about in a bottle. Christianity without tears—that's what soma is."

"你可以在瓶子里装着你道德的一半。" 这句话的分量在于：它许诺的不是舒适，是可携带。而任何被优化成可携带的东西，都已经脱离了产生它的条件。

列宁娜对伯纳德说这段更直接，小说 p.91：

"Why don't you take soma when you have these dreadful ideas of yours. You'd forget all about them. And instead of being miserable, you'd be jolly." （你有这些可怕的想法时，为什么不吃点 soma 呢？你会把它们全忘掉。然后你就不痛苦了，会很快乐。）

注意她给的理由：不是因为它对，是因为它会让你不再不舒服。

这就是有限游戏的理想终局：没有人输，没有人赢，游戏在所有人都不再想玩的时候结束。 而它不需要暴力——小说里林达是被 soma 反复使用耗死的，但这不是"被敌人杀死"，是"被自己选择的舒适消耗掉"。

今天的技术叙事大量是这种形状的。 它承诺的不是你变强，是你不必再面对那些让你难受的东西。便利、自动化、"不用再自己判断了"——每一条都在降低你需要进入游戏的可能性。

"The power of holding two contradictory beliefs in one's mind simultaneously, and accepting both of them." （同时在脑中持有两种矛盾信念，并且同时接受两者的力量。）

奥威尔自己给出的三个互斥项是：战争即和平、自由即奴役、无知即力量。

关键在定义里的那个限定词：held in one's mind。 双重思维不是"被强迫相信谎言"——那是有痛苦有抵抗的。双重思维是同时持有互斥的表述，而它们不被允许相遇。两个命题都在场，但从不并置在一个焦点上。

这在形式上正是"保证游戏不结束的规则"。 奥威尔的小说里，党的口号自相矛盾，而党在形式上是不可击败的——因为任何一次真正的对质都会让矛盾被同时看见，而那正是党要避免的事。

V. Two Endgames

The finite game has two ways of dying, and they look completely different. This is the difference between Huxley and Orwell, and the most practical distinction in this essay.

Huxley: let everyone voluntarily stop playing

In Brave New World (1932), soma does not make people stop suffering; it makes people no longer need the capacity for suffering at all. About its mechanism there is this passage (Chapter 17, Mustapha Mond to John):

"And there's always soma to calm your anger, to reconcile you to your enemies, to make you patient and long-suffering. [...] Anybody can be virtuous now. You can carry at least half of your morality about in a bottle. Christianity without tears—that's what soma is."

"You can carry at least half of your morality about in a bottle." The weight of this sentence is that what it promises is not comfort but portability. And anything optimized to be portable has already been detached from the conditions that produced it.

Lenina says it more directly to Bernard, novel p.91:

"Why don't you take soma when you have these dreadful ideas of yours. You'd forget all about them. And instead of being miserable, you'd be jolly."

Note the reason she gives: not because it is right, but because it will make you stop feeling uncomfortable.

This is the ideal endgame of the finite game: no one loses, no one wins, the game ends when no one wants to play anymore. And it does not need violence—in the novel, Linda is worn out by repeated soma, but that is not "killed by an enemy," it is "consumed by the comfort one chose oneself."

Today's technological narratives are largely this shape. What they promise is not that you will become strong, but that you no longer have to face what makes you uncomfortable. Convenience, automation, "you no longer have to judge for yourself"—each one lowers the probability that you will need to enter the game.

Orwell: never ending, and never advancing

In Nineteen Eighty-Four (1949), Big Brother acts on the body, the Thought Police act on behavior, and Newspeak acts on language itself. The definition of doublethink (Part I, Chapter 3) is:

"The power of holding two contradictory beliefs in one's mind simultaneously, and accepting both of them."

The three mutually exclusive terms Orwell himself gives are: war is peace, freedom is slavery, ignorance is strength.

The key is the qualifier in the definition: held in one's mind. Doublethink is not "being forced to believe lies"—that involves suffering and resistance. Doublethink is holding two mutually exclusive statements at the same time, while they are not allowed to meet. Both propositions are present, but they are never placed in a single focus.

Formally, this is exactly "the rules that guarantee the game never ends." In Orwell's novel, the Party's slogans are self-contradictory, and the Party is formally unbeatable—because any genuine confrontation would let the contradiction be seen at once, and that is precisely what the Party avoids.

The practical difference between the two deaths

Huxley Orwell
Player state does not want to play anymore still playing, and thinks it is progressing
Rules rules serve "players exit" rules serve "never end and never advance"
Danger players voluntarily abandon judgment players voluntarily abandon sensitivity to contradiction
Who benefits the side maintaining order the side maintaining the narrative

Note the difference on the second row: Huxley's danger is that players exit the game; Orwell's danger is that players stay in the game and are playing with gusto. The latter is harder to recognize, because it looks like progress.

And I have to acknowledge a fact that is unfavorable to me: in writing this essay I am using precisely Orwell's mechanism. "We are fighting the finite-game war with infinite-game technology"—if this sentence holds, it is also a juxtaposition: a mutually exclusive structure (the shape of the technology / the shape of the organization) held at the same time without confrontation. I did not let them meet; I merely arranged them in a row.

六、阿西莫夫的修憲

最後是律條。

1941 年 10 月，阿西莫夫寫了《Runaround》，1942 年 3 月刊於《Astounding Science-Fiction》——機器人系列第三篇。三條定律首次出現在這裡：

  1. A robot may not injure a human being, or through action or inaction allow a human being to come to harm.
  2. A robot must obey the orders given by human beings except where such orders conflict with the First Law.
  3. A robot must protect its own existence except where this protection conflicts with the First or Second Law.

這是一個純粹的有限遊戲裝置。 三條規則，規則的目的寫得很清楚：保證衝突有一個可判定的解決順序，從而保證遊戲總能得到一個終局。第一條是絕對底線，第二條讓出，第二條讓出自己。

而《Runaround》這個篇名是雙關：runaround 意為"躲閃、兜圈子"，正是小說裡那臺機器人的失控機制——它不斷違反第一定律，而違反的方式是徹底不動。 有限遊戲的一條邊界被推到極端，結果不是更自由，是卡死。

然後是真正有意思的部分。

後來，阿西莫夫補了"第零定律"：機器人不得因行為傷害人類，除非傷害人類與執行某條更高層指令衝突。這條後加的定律後來成了《基地》系列的核心。

我的讀法（這是本文的解讀，不是原文的斷言）：第零定律做的事，在形式上是換了邊界。從"某個具體的人不可被傷害"變成"人類整體的利益優先"，而人類整體不在任何既有規則列表裡——它是一個規則無法預設的目標。

也就是說：把遊戲繼續玩下去所需要的那個修改，恰好是一次從有限遊戲到無限遊戲的轉換。 而它是以修正案的形式發生的，發生在律條生效幾十年之後。

這就是我這一篇最想說的一句關於律條的話：

任何完備的有限遊戲規則集，最終都需要一條它自己無法寫出的規則才能繼續。而這條規則不會以"規則"的形式被接受——它只能以"優先"、"例外"、"更高層"這種形式被偷偷加進來。

而我們今天正在看著這件事重演。"安全第一""可解釋性優先""人類在環"——每一句都是一個新的優先項，都是在把邊界往外挪。 它們都不是從"遊戲要贏"這個目標裡推出來的，它們是遊戲太長、玩家太累、或者有人對規則不滿意的時候被加進來的。

"人類在環"到底是什麼？ 它是一句很短的規則。它的意思不是"人類永遠在場"——如果在場不是否決權，那在場只是一個觀眾席。它真正的意思是"人類在最後的節點上仍然持有單方面結束這次交互的能力"。 這是一個結構性的位置，不是一句承諾。

六、阿西莫夫的修宪

最后是律条。

1941 年 10 月，阿西莫夫写了《Runaround》，1942 年 3 月刊于《Astounding Science-Fiction》——机器人系列第三篇。三条定律首次出现在这里：

  1. A robot may not injure a human being, or through action or inaction allow a human being to come to harm.
  2. A robot must obey the orders given by human beings except where such orders conflict with the First Law.
  3. A robot must protect its own existence except where this protection conflicts with the First or Second Law.

这是一个纯粹的有限游戏装置。 三条规则，规则的目的写得很清楚：保证冲突有一个可判定的解决顺序，从而保证游戏总能得到一个终局。第一条是绝对底线，第二条让出，第二条让出自己。

而《Runaround》这个篇名是双关：runaround 意为"躲闪、兜圈子"，正是小说里那台机器人的失控机制——它不断违反第一定律，而违反的方式是彻底不动。 有限游戏的一条边界被推到极端，结果不是更自由，是卡死。

然后是真正有意思的部分。

后来，阿西莫夫补了"第零定律"：机器人不得因行为伤害人类，除非伤害人类与执行某条更高层指令冲突。这条后加的定律后来成了《基地》系列的核心。

我的读法（这是本文的解读，不是原文的断言）：第零定律做的事，在形式上是换了边界。从"某个具体的人不可被伤害"变成"人类整体的利益优先"，而人类整体不在任何既有规则列表里——它是一个规则无法预设的目标。

也就是说：把游戏继续玩下去所需要的那个修改，恰好是一次从有限游戏到无限游戏的转换。 而它是以修正案的形式发生的，发生在律条生效几十年之后。

这就是我这一篇最想说的一句关于律条的话：

任何完备的有限游戏规则集，最终都需要一条它自己无法写出的规则才能继续。而这条规则不会以"规则"的形式被接受——它只能以"优先"、"例外"、"更高层"这种形式被偷偷加进来。

而我们今天正在看着这件事重演。"安全第一""可解释性优先""人类在环"——每一句都是一个新的优先项，都是在把边界往外挪。 它们都不是从"游戏要赢"这个目标里推出来的，它们是游戏太长、玩家太累、或者有人对规则不满意的时候被加进来的。

"人类在环"到底是什么？ 它是一句很短的规则。它的意思不是"人类永远在场"——如果在场不是否决权，那在场只是一个观众席。它真正的意思是"人类在最后的节点上仍然持有单方面结束这次交互的能力"。 这是一个结构性的位置，不是一句承诺。

VI. Asimov's Constitutional Amendment

Last, the laws.

In October 1941, Asimov wrote "Runaround"; it was published in March 1942 in Astounding Science-Fiction—the third story in the robot series. The Three Laws first appear here:

  1. A robot may not injure a human being, or through action or inaction allow a human being to come to harm.
  2. A robot must obey the orders given by human beings except where such orders conflict with the First Law.
  3. A robot must protect its own existence except where this protection conflicts with the First or Second Law.

This is a pure finite-game device. Three rules, and the purpose of the rules is written clearly: to guarantee that conflicts have a decidable order of resolution, and thus that the game can always reach an endgame. The first law is the absolute bottom line; the second yields; the second yields itself.

And the title "Runaround" is a pun: runaround means "dodging, going in circles," which is exactly the runaway mechanism of the robot in the story—it keeps violating the First Law, and the way it does so is by staying completely still. A finite game's boundary pushed to the extreme does not yield more freedom; it yields deadlock.

Then comes the really interesting part.

Later, Asimov added the "Zeroth Law": a robot must not harm humanity through action or inaction, except where such harm conflicts with carrying out a higher-order directive. This after-added law later became the core of the Foundation series.

My reading (this is my interpretation in this essay, not a claim of the original): what the Zeroth Law does, formally, is change the boundary. From "a specific individual may not be harmed" to "the interests of humanity as a whole take priority," and humanity as a whole is in no existing list of rules—it is a goal that no rule can pre-suppose.

That is to say: the modification needed to keep the game going is precisely a move from the finite game to the infinite game. And it happened in the form of an amendment, decades after the laws took effect.

This is the one sentence about the laws I most want to say in this essay:

Any complete set of finite-game rules ultimately needs one rule that it itself cannot write, in order to continue. And that rule will not be accepted in the form of a "rule"—it can only be slipped in in the form of a "priority," an "exception," or a "higher level."

And we are watching this replay today. "Safety first." "Explainability first." "Human in the loop."—each is a new priority, each is moving the boundary outward. None of them is derived from the goal of "winning the game"; they are added when the game has gone on too long, when the players are too tired, or when someone is unhappy with the rules.

What exactly is "human in the loop"? It is a very short rule. Its meaning is not that "humans are always present"—if presence were not a veto power, presence would just be a spectator stand. Its real meaning is "a human retains, at the final node, the ability to unilaterally end this interaction." That is a structural position, not a promise.

七、馬克思：異化是有限遊戲的政治表述

在把這一切收進一個框架之前，我要處理一個我上一稿用得很重的概念，因為它在新框架裡的位置變清晰了。

馬克思的異化勞動（Entfremdung，《1844 年經濟學哲學手稿》"異化勞動"節）通常被拆成四個維度：

維度 原文命題
與勞動產品異化 工人越生產，產品的世界越強大，而工人自身越貧乏
與勞動過程異化 "他在勞動中感到自己在自己之外"（bei der Arbeit außer sich）
與類本質異化 人的類存在是自由自覺的創造性活動，被降格為動物性的生存需要
與他人異化 "直接後果……是，人與人的相互關係被異化了"（unmittelbare Folge）

前三條是馬克思明確列舉的。 第四條他用的是"直接後果"（unmittelbare Folge），後世常把四條並列為同等地位。這個次序本身有解釋力：異化不是四個並列的症狀，它是先在人與物之間發生，再從那裡蔓延到人與人之間。

在新框架裡，這四個維度得到一個更簡潔的說明。 按馬克思的用法，人的勞動活動（Lebenstätigkeit，生命活動）是典型的無限遊戲——它沒有終點，它的產物是能力本身而不是勝利。而僱傭勞動是一個有限遊戲：它有明確終點（工時）、明確勝負（工資與剩餘價值）、明確規則（不許帶走、必須交付）。

異化不是"人被剝削"這麼籠統的東西。異化是一個無限遊戲被裝進一個有限遊戲的框。 四個維度是這件事在四個位置上的顯影：

  • 產品被拿走 → 你的行為產物成了別人的勝負記分牌
  • 過程變成手段 → 一個本該是目的的活動，被規定為有終點
  • 類本質降格 → 你在其中發展出來的能力，不計入你的分數
  • 與他人異化 → 其他玩家也各自在框裡，所以互相是對手

這個讀法不是馬克思的。它是我的。 但它有一個好處：它能解釋為什麼"提高工資"不能解決異化——提高工資只是把有限遊戲的分數調高，沒有改變這是一場有限遊戲。 而馬克思說異化的解決方式是取消僱傭勞動本身，即取消那個框。

而這裡有一個我必須點破的對應：如果僱傭勞動是一個有限遊戲，那麼今天"用 AI 替代一部分勞動"在形式上是什麼？

按這個框架，它不是"取消框"，是換框：把一部分活動從有限遊戲裡拿出來，放進一個沒有終點的循環裡。而被放出來的那部分活動，如果它原本是人的"生命活動"（學習、判斷、形成能力），那它現在進入了一個無法產出分數的處境。

無法產出分數不是自由。沒有分數的活動，在大多數人的現實處境裡，等於沒有被承認。 而一個不被承認的活動，人會自己給它造一個分數——最常見的兩個是"效率"和"產量"。

於是，有限遊戲回來了。而且是通過最迂迴的方式回來的：它以"我不再給這件事打分"的姿態進場，然後在真空裡被自己填滿。

七、马克思：异化是有限游戏的政治表述

在把这一切收进一个框架之前，我要处理一个我上一稿用得很重的概念，因为它在新框架里的位置变清晰了。

马克思的异化劳动（Entfremdung，《1844 年经济学哲学手稿》"异化劳动"节）通常被拆成四个维度：

维度 原文命题
与劳动产品异化 工人越生产，产品的世界越强大，而工人自身越贫乏
与劳动过程异化 "他在劳动中感到自己在自己之外"（bei der Arbeit außer sich）
与类本质异化 人的类存在是自由自觉的创造性活动，被降格为动物性的生存需要
与他人异化 "直接后果……是，人与人的相互关系被异化了"（unmittelbare Folge）

前三条是马克思明确列举的。 第四条他用的是"直接后果"（unmittelbare Folge），后世常把四条并列为同等地位。这个次序本身有解释力：异化不是四个并列的症状，它是先在人与物之间发生，再从那里蔓延到人与人之间。

在新框架里，这四个维度得到一个更简洁的说明。 按马克思的用法，人的劳动活动（Lebenstätigkeit，生命活动）是典型的无限游戏——它没有终点，它的产物是能力本身而不是胜利。而雇佣劳动是一个有限游戏：它有明确终点（工时）、明确胜负（工资与剩余价值）、明确规则（不许带走、必须交付）。

异化不是"人被剥削"这么笼统的东西。异化是一个无限游戏被装进一个有限游戏的框。 四个维度是这件事在四个位置上的显影：

  • 产品被拿走 → 你的行为产物成了别人的胜负记分牌
  • 过程变成手段 → 一个本该是目的的活动，被规定为有终点
  • 类本质降格 → 你在其中发展出来的能力，不计入你的分数
  • 与他人异化 → 其他玩家也各自在框里，所以互相是对手

这个读法不是马克思的。它是我的。 但它有一个好处：它能解释为什么"提高工资"不能解决异化——提高工资只是把有限游戏的分数调高，没有改变这是一场有限游戏。 而马克思说异化的解决方式是取消雇佣劳动本身，即取消那个框。

而这里有一个我必须点破的对应：如果雇佣劳动是一个有限游戏，那么今天"用 AI 替代一部分劳动"在形式上是什么？

按这个框架，它不是"取消框"，是换框：把一部分活动从有限游戏里拿出来，放进一个没有终点的循环里。而被放出来的那部分活动，如果它原本是人的"生命活动"（学习、判断、形成能力），那它现在进入了一个无法产出分数的处境。

无法产出分数不是自由。没有分数的活动，在大多数人的现实处境里，等于没有被承认。 而一个不被承认的活动，人会自己给它造一个分数——最常见的两个是"效率"和"产量"。

于是，有限游戏回来了。而且是通过最迂回的方式回来的：它以"我不再给这件事打分"的姿态进场，然后在真空里被自己填满。

VII. Marx: Alienation as the Political Form of the Finite Game

Before I fold all of this into one frame, I have to deal with a concept I leaned on heavily in my last draft, because its position in the new frame has become clearer.

Marx's alienated labor (Entfremdung, the "Alienated Labor" section of the Economic and Philosophic Manuscripts of 1844) is usually broken into four dimensions:

Dimension Source proposition
Alienation from the product of labor the more the worker produces, the more powerful the world of products becomes, and the poorer the worker himself
Alienation from the labor process "in labor he feels himself outside himself" (bei der Arbeit außer sich)
Alienation from species-being man's species-existence is free, self-conscious creative activity, reduced to an animal need for survival
Alienation from others "the immediate consequence … is that man's relations with other men are alienated" (unmittelbare Folge)

The first three are explicitly listed by Marx. For the fourth he uses "immediate consequence" (unmittelbare Folge); later readers often set all four on an equal footing. The ordering itself is explanatory: alienation does not arise as four parallel symptoms; it happens first between man and thing, and spreads from there to relations among men.

In the new frame, the four dimensions get a cleaner reading. By Marx's usage, human labor activity (Lebenstätigkeit, life-activity) is the paradigmatic infinite game—it has no endpoint, and its product is capability itself rather than victory. Wage labor is a finite game: it has a clear endpoint (hours), a clear win/lose (wage vs. surplus value), and clear rules (you may not take anything away, you must hand it over).

Alienation is not some vague "the person is exploited." Alienation is an infinite game stuffed into the frame of a finite game. The four dimensions are the way this shows up in four places:

  • The product is taken away → your product of action becomes someone else's scoreboard
  • The process becomes a means → an activity that ought to be an end is stipulated to have an endpoint
  • Species-being is downgraded → the capability you develop within it does not count toward your score
  • Alienation from others → every other player is likewise inside a frame, so you are mutually opponents

This reading is not Marx's. It is mine. But it has one advantage: it explains why "raising the wage" cannot cure alienation—raising the wage only turns up the score of the finite game; it does not change the fact that it is a finite game. Marx says the cure for alienation is to abolish wage labor itself, i.e. to abolish the frame.

And here is a correspondence I must point out: if wage labor is a finite game, then what is "using AI to replace part of labor" today, formally?

By this frame, it is not "abolishing the frame"; it is swapping the frame: taking some activities out of the finite game and putting them into a loop with no endpoint. And that portion of activities, if it was originally the person's "life-activity" (learning, judging, forming capability), now enters a situation in which it cannot produce a score.

Cannot produce a score is not freedom. An activity without a score is, in most people's actual circumstances, an activity that goes unrecognized. And an activity that is not recognized is one for which people invent a score themselves—the two most common are "efficiency" and "output."

So the finite game returns. And it returns the most circuitous way possible: it enters wearing the mask of "I no longer grade this," and then fills the vacuum with itself.

八、我的系統：一臺偽裝成無限遊戲的有限機器

我把自己的系統拿出來，而且要拿出最難看的部分。

我同時用兩個智能體：本地那個只做探測和上報，不下判斷；雲端那個做研判，但不能自己醒，必須等一份觸發文件；所有審批由人籤。

從 Carse 的框架看，這套系統的形狀是什麼？

它有一個循環（探測→喚醒→研判→執行→日誌→等待），而且這個循環不由任何一方宣佈結束。形式上它非常像無限遊戲。

但它有三個明確寫著的終止條件：本地那個不能自己下判斷（能力被剝奪）；雲端那個不能自己醒（不能自啟動）；審批永遠由人籤（人保留單方面結束權）。這三條不是我的隱喻，是系統憲法裡寫死的約束。

這三條約束是什麼？

它們是三條有限遊戲的邊界。它們規定了什麼不能發生（本地不能判斷）、什麼不能自主發生（雲端不能啟動）、什麼必須由人在場（審批）。

所以這套系統的真實結構是：外形是無限遊戲（一個自我延續的循環），而內部寫死了三條有限遊戲的邊界。 而把它稱為"無限遊戲"，是在用外形掩蓋內部——這正是我在第六節批評的奧威爾式機制，用在了我自己的系統上。

更難看的一層。

這套系統裡有一個我寫過"審或不審"的節點。探測規則明確、觸發條件明確、邊界明確。只有在這個節點上，整個系統沒有任何可被寫下來的判定依據。 我可以有一套觸發條件的描述，但我沒有一套"何時該說"的語法。

那是不是說這個節點是唯一的自由？

我上一稿說是。現在我認為不是。"自由"是錯的詞。 那個節點上真實的處境是有限：我不知道我不知道什麼。每一次"審或不審"都是在一個我無法窮盡的錯誤集合裡取一個樣本。那不是自由，是責任。 把它說成自由，是我給自己發的一張獎狀。

而第三層，是最難看的一層：我知道這套系統有兩個自我延續的機制。

一個是這條 agent 循環。另一個是這套循環在為我生產的東西——文章、卡片、決策草稿。第二個循環也有一個特徵：它不由我宣佈結束，我停下來的方式是"不看了"。

兩個都不由我宣佈結束。這很誘人。但我要小心。 因為一個不由我宣佈結束的東西，很可能只是一個我沒有找到結束它的理由的東西。這兩者的區別在外觀上完全沒有。Carse 說"如果不再可能有意外，所有的遊戲都停止"——而我可以誠實地說：我不知道我這套系統裡還有沒有意外會產生。這就是我目前的處境，也是我為什麼用一隻石斧而不是一把更精密的工具來結句。

石斧是什麼？

它是一件極其有限、極其明確的工具：只有一道刃、只有一個用途、沒有任何可配置項。 它沒有排行榜，不能被升級，不會取代任何東西。

而有限性在這裡不是缺陷，是唯一能防止我剛才那三層自我粉飾的東西。 一件不可配置的工具，它的使用方式是可以被窮舉的，所以我無法在用它的時候"順便做點別的"。

我用它，不是因為它先進。是因為它不可審計的地方最少。

八、我的系统：一台伪装成无限游戏的有限机器

我把自己的系统拿出来，而且要拿出最难看的部分。

我同时用两个智能体：本地那个只做探测和上报，不下判断；云端那个做研判，但不能自己醒，必须等一份触发文件；所有审批由人签。

从 Carse 的框架看，这套系统的形状是什么？

它有一个循环（探测→唤醒→研判→执行→日志→等待），而且这个循环不由任何一方宣布结束。形式上它非常像无限游戏。

但它有三个明确写着的终止条件：本地那个不能自己下判断（能力被剥夺）；云端那个不能自己醒（不能自启动）；审批永远由人签（人保留单方面结束权）。这三条不是我的隐喻，是系统宪法里写死的约束。

这三条约束是什么？

它们是三条有限游戏的边界。它们规定了什么不能发生（本地不能判断）、什么不能自主发生（云端不能启动）、什么必须由人在场（审批）。

所以这套系统的真实结构是：外形是无限游戏（一个自我延续的循环），而内部写死了三条有限游戏的边界。 而把它称为"无限游戏"，是在用外形掩盖内部——这正是我在第六节批评的奥威尔式机制，用在了我自己的系统上。

更难看的一层。

这套系统里有一个我写过"审或不审"的节点。探测规则明确、触发条件明确、边界明确。只有在这个节点上，整个系统没有任何可被写下来的判定依据。 我可以有一套触发条件的描述，但我没有一套"何时该说"的语法。

那是不是说这个节点是唯一的自由？

我上一稿说是。现在我认为不是。"自由"是错的词。 那个节点上真实的处境是有限：我不知道我不知道什么。每一次"审或不审"都是在一个我无法穷尽的错误集合里取一个样本。那不是自由，是责任。 把它说成自由，是我给自己发的一张奖状。

而第三层，是最难看的一层：我知道这套系统有两个自我延续的机制。

一个是这条 agent 循环。另一个是这套循环在为我生产的东西——文章、卡片、决策草稿。第二个循环也有一个特征：它不由我宣布结束，我停下来的方式是"不看了"。

两个都不由我宣布结束。这很诱人。但我要小心。 因为一个不由我宣布结束的东西，很可能只是一个我没有找到结束它的理由的东西。这两者的区别在外观上完全没有。Carse 说"如果不再可能有意外，所有的游戏都停止"——而我可以诚实地说：我不知道我这套系统里还有没有意外会产生。这就是我目前的处境，也是我为什么用一只石斧而不是一把更精密的工具来结句。

石斧是什么？

它是一件极其有限、极其明确的工具：只有一道刃、只有一个用途、没有任何可配置项。 它没有排行榜，不能被升级，不会取代任何东西。

而有限性在这里不是缺陷，是唯一能防止我刚才那三层自我粉饰的东西。 一件不可配置的工具，它的使用方式是可以被穷举的，所以我无法在用它的时候"顺便做点别的"。

我用它，不是因为它先进。是因为它不可审计的地方最少。

VIII. My System: A Finite Machine Disguised as an Infinite Game

I bring out my own system, and the ugliest part of it.

I run two agents at once: the local one only probes and reports; it does not judge. The cloud one judges, but it cannot wake itself; it must wait for a trigger file. All approvals are signed by a human.

From Carse's frame, what is the shape of this system?

It has a loop (probe → wake → judge → execute → log → wait), and no party declares the loop finished. Formally it looks very much like an infinite game.

But it has three termination conditions that are explicitly written down: the local one may not judge on its own (capability stripped away); the cloud one may not wake itself (no self-start); approvals are always signed by a human (the human retains the unilateral power to end). These three are not my metaphors; they are hard constraints written into the system's constitution.

What are these three constraints?

They are three finite-game boundaries. They stipulate what may not happen (the local one may not judge), what may not happen on its own (the cloud one may not start), and what must have a human present (approval).

So the real structure of this system is: the exterior is the shape of an infinite game (a self-perpetuating loop), while three finite-game boundaries are hard-wired inside. And calling it an "infinite game" is using the exterior to cover up the interior—which is exactly the Orwellian mechanism I criticized in section six, applied to my own system.

An uglier layer.

This system has a node where I wrote "judge it or not judge it." The probing rule is clear, the trigger condition is clear, the boundary is clear. Only at this node does the whole system have no written criterion of judgment. I can have a description of the trigger conditions, but I do not have a grammar of "when to speak."

So does that make this node the only freedom?

My last draft said yes. Now I think no. "Freedom" is the wrong word. The actual situation at that node is finitude: I do not know what I do not know. Every "judge it or not" is drawing a sample from a set of errors I cannot exhaust. That is not freedom; it is responsibility. To call it freedom is a certificate I issued to myself.

And the third layer, the ugliest: I know this system has two self-perpetuating mechanisms.

One is this agent loop. The other is what this loop produces for me—articles, cards, decision drafts. The second loop has one feature too: I do not declare it finished; the way I stop is "I stop reading."

Neither of the two is ended by my declaration. That is seductive. But I must be careful. Because a thing I have not found a reason to end is very likely merely a thing I have not yet found the reason to end. The two look exactly alike on the outside. Carse says "if surprise is no longer possible, all play ceases"—and I can honestly say: I do not know whether my system still produces surprises. That is my present situation, and it is why I close with a stone axe rather than a more precise tool.

What is the stone axe?

It is an extremely finite, extremely explicit tool: one edge, one use, no configuration options at all. It has no leaderboard, cannot be upgraded, and replaces nothing.

Here finitude is not a defect; it is the most minimal thing that can stop the three layers of self-flattery I just described. A non-configurable tool has a usage that can be enumerated, so I cannot "do something else on the side" while using it.

I use it not because it is advanced, but because it leaves the least that cannot be audited.

九、蘇格拉底會問的三個問題

如果蘇格拉底今天推門進來，他不會去質問那些硅谷的巨頭，他會指著我的屏幕，問我三個問題。

第一。 你說神話命名是一種減震器。但為什麼世界上那麼多人用著叫 Llama（羊駝）、DeepSeek（深度求索）、Qwen（千問）的系統？你是不是把極客趣味與商標註冊的妥協，當成了全人類的本體論危機？

第二。 你說你的系統裡沒有"我"，因為你剝奪了本地跑腿者的判斷力。可"什麼時候喚醒雲端"這條規則本身難道不是判斷？把因果鏈切碎、然後聲稱每一環都無辜，這不正是官僚體制逃避責任最古老的手段嗎？

第三。 你說你在系統裡沒有被異化，因為你擁有完全的理解與拒絕權。可你今天在屏幕前坐了十幾個小時，為了讓輸出可用而不斷改寫自己的提問方式，為了防止出錯而當著全天候質檢員——究竟是系統成了你的延伸，還是你成了這臺分佈式機器上最高級、也最廉價的一塊肉身伺服器？

這三個問題我一個也推不掉。

而且第三個問題在我這次的新框架下變得更難回答了。上一稿我可以說"我沒被異化，因為我還持有判斷權"。現在我可以說的是：持有判斷權和在玩一場需要延續的遊戲，是兩件不同的事。 一個人可以完全保留否決權，同時正在做一件他沒有在做、只是在延續的事。

這兩個問題不能靠引用解答。 它們必須靠繼續追問來維持——而"繼續追問"這件事，恰好是這一篇的主題。

九、苏格拉底会问的三个问题

如果苏格拉底今天推门进来，他不会去质问那些硅谷的巨头，他会指着我的屏幕，问我三个问题。

第一。 你说神话命名是一种减震器。但为什么世界上那么多人用着叫 Llama（羊驼）、DeepSeek（深度求索）、Qwen（千问）的系统？你是不是把极客趣味与商标注册的妥协，当成了全人类的本体论危机？

第二。 你说你的系统里没有"我"，因为你剥夺了本地跑腿者的判断力。可"什么时候唤醒云端"这条规则本身难道不是判断？把因果链切碎、然后声称每一环都无辜，这不正是官僚体制逃避责任最古老的手段吗？

第三。 你说你在系统里没有被异化，因为你拥有完全的理解与拒绝权。可你今天在屏幕前坐了十几个小时，为了让输出可用而不断改写自己的提问方式，为了防止出错而当着全天候质检员——究竟是系统成了你的延伸，还是你成了这台分布式机器上最高级、也最廉价的一块肉身伺服器？

这三个问题我一个也推不掉。

而且第三个问题在我这次的新框架下变得更难回答了。上一稿我可以说"我没被异化，因为我还持有判断权"。现在我可以说的是：持有判断权和在玩一场需要延续的游戏，是两件不同的事。 一个人可以完全保留否决权，同时正在做一件他没有在做、只是在延续的事。

这两个问题不能靠引用解答。 它们必须靠继续追问来维持——而"继续追问"这件事，恰好是这一篇的主题。

IX. Three Questions Socrates Would Ask

If Socrates walked in the door today, he would not go interrogate the Silicon Valley giants; he would point at my screen and ask me three questions.

First. You say that mythic naming is a shock absorber. But why do so many people in the world use systems called Llama (llama), DeepSeek (deep seeking), Qwen (thousand questions)? Have you mistaken a geek's whimsy and a trademark compromise for the whole of humanity's ontological crisis?

Second. You say there is no "I" in your system, because you stripped the local errand-runner of its judgment. But is the rule "when to wake the cloud" itself not a judgment? To shatter the causal chain and then claim each link is innocent—is that not the oldest way a bureaucracy evades responsibility?

Third. You say you are not alienated in your system, because you retain full understanding and the right to refuse. But today you sat before the screen for over ten hours, constantly rewriting the way you ask so that the output would be usable, acting as a round-the-clock quality inspector so as not to err—did the system become your extension, or did you become the most sophisticated and the most cheaply replaceable piece of flesh serving this distributed machine?

I cannot duck any of these three.

And the third is harder to answer under the new frame I have now. In my last draft I could say "I am not alienated, because I still hold the right to judge." What I can now say is: holding the right to judge and playing a game that must continue are two different things. A person can keep the veto power completely, while at the same time doing something that is not being done, merely being continued.

Neither question can be answered by quotation. They must be kept alive by continuing to press—and "continuing to press" is precisely the subject of this essay.

結語

先交代這篇文章自己的形式：它沒有給出結論。

不是"我暫時沒有答案"那種收尾。是更具體的一件事：這一篇的結構選擇本身就是一個有限遊戲。 我用七節來論證一個區分，然後在第八節承認我的實踐不符合這個區分。如果我的論證成立，它應該也能解釋我為什麼會想要一個不算計的結論。 我解釋不了。所以我不給。

我留下的只有三句。

第一句是 Carse 的，也是這本系列最適用的一句：只有一種無限遊戲，而它是生命本身。 所有"我們要贏得這場技術競賽"的說法，都默認了另一場遊戲的存在。那場遊戲是誰在玩、什麼時候結束、誰在記分——這個問題我們從來沒問過，因為問它的人看起來像在拖後腿。

第二句關於律條：任何完備的有限遊戲規則集，最終都需要一條它自己寫不出的規則才能繼續。 而這條規則不會以規則的形式被接受，它會以"優先""例外""人類在環"的形式被加進去。加進去的時候沒人說這是邊界移動，大家說這是"更負責任"。

第三句關於我自己：我有一套系統，外形是一個不會自己結束的循環，內部寫死了三條有限遊戲的邊界，而我沒有辦法判斷哪一個才是真的那個。

我判斷不了，所以我寫下來了。 而這件事本身，也許就是"唯一的無限遊戲是生命"這句話在一個人身上能留下的唯一痕跡：

它不是一個結論。它是一個形狀。而你只能在往下寫的過程中發現它是什麼。

结语

先交代这篇文章自己的形式：它没有给出结论。

不是"我暂时没有答案"那种收尾。是更具体的一件事：这一篇的结构选择本身就是一个有限游戏。 我用七节来论证一个区分，然后在第八节承认我的实践不符合这个区分。如果我的论证成立，它应该也能解释我为什么会想要一个不算计的结论。 我解释不了。所以我不给。

我留下的只有三句。

第一句是 Carse 的，也是这本系列最适用的一句：只有一种无限游戏，而它是生命本身。 所有"我们要赢得这场技术竞赛"的说法，都默认了另一场游戏的存在。那场游戏是谁在玩、什么时候结束、谁在记分——这个问题我们从来没问过，因为问它的人看起来像在拖后腿。

第二句关于律条：任何完备的有限游戏规则集，最终都需要一条它自己写不出的规则才能继续。 而这条规则不会以规则的形式被接受，它会以"优先""例外""人类在环"的形式被加进去。加进去的时候没人说这是边界移动，大家说这是"更负责任"。

第三句关于我自己：我有一套系统，外形是一个不会自己结束的循环，内部写死了三条有限游戏的边界，而我没有办法判断哪一个才是真的那个。

我判断不了，所以我写下来了。 而这件事本身，也许就是"唯一的无限游戏是生命"这句话在一个人身上能留下的唯一痕迹：

它不是一个结论。它是一个形状。而你只能在往下写的过程中发现它是什么。

Epilogue

First, a word about this essay's own form: it does not offer a conclusion.

Not the kind of close that says "I have no answer yet." It is a more specific thing: the structural choice of this essay itself is a finite game. I use seven sections to argue for a distinction, and then in the eighth section admit that my practice does not match that distinction. If my argument holds, it should also be able to explain why I would want a conclusion that does not count. I cannot explain it. So I do not give one.

What I leave is only three sentences.

The first is Carse's, and the most fitting for this series: there is but one infinite game, and it is life itself. Every statement of "we must win this technology race" presupposes the existence of another game. Who is playing that game, when does it end, who is keeping score—this question we have never asked, because asking it looks like dragging the team down.

The second is about the laws: any complete set of finite-game rules ultimately needs one rule that it itself cannot write, in order to continue. And that rule is not accepted in the form of a rule; it is added in the form of a "priority," an "exception," a "human in the loop." When it is added, no one says this is a movement of the boundary; everyone says it is "more responsible."

The third is about myself: I have a system whose exterior is a loop that does not end by itself, whose interior has three finite-game boundaries hard-wired, and I have no way to tell which is the real one.

I cannot judge, so I wrote it down. And that act itself may be the only trace that "the only infinite game is life" can leave in a single person:

It is not a conclusion. It is a shape. And you can only discover what it is in the act of writing on.
