一、終端裡坐著兩個神話

先看一個我每天在用的系統。

我的 Mac 上常駐一個進程，叫 Hermes。它每三十分鐘醒一次，讀一個代碼倉庫的變更列表，逐條比對，命中就記一筆，沒命中就重新睡回去。它不思考，不判斷，不寫文章。它做的事是：讓一臺機器以零邊際成本，替人反覆看同一批文件。

在硅谷 Meta 託管的一臺雲端虛擬機裡，坐著另一個模型，叫 Muse。它每週有固定份額，用完即止，週日零點重置不結轉。它平時全靜默，不主動消耗任何東西——直到某天 Hermes 掃到一條標記為"提問"的事項，才寫下一個觸發文件把它叫醒。它的活是隻讀研判、合規審查、跨庫的事實裁定。它是這套系統裡唯一有資格說"不"的角色。

一低一高。一跑腿一審判。一個本地零成本，一個雲端按周計費。這不是設計出來的隱喻，這是資源約束的結果：貴資源只能用在貴問題上。

但有意思的地方不在架構。

有意思的地方在於，當 Hermes 在日誌裡寫下那份觸發文件時，那個名字已經不是產品名了，它是崗位職責說明書。

赫爾墨斯，希臘神話裡穿飛鞋的信使，唯一能在天界、人間、冥府之間自由穿行的角色。西方語言裡"詮釋學"（hermeneutics）的詞根也是他——神話裡負責把不可說的翻譯成可說的。

而我這個進程乾的活，翻譯過來正好是：跨系統搬運、長文本轉譯、按既定規則來回跑。

繆斯，希臘神話裡掌管文藝與靈感的女神群，繆斯九女。文藝、史詩、幾何、天文各有其主。

而我這個雲端模型乾的活，翻譯過來正好是：判斷、裁定、仲裁。

我沒有為它們取名。名字是 Anthropic 取的 Claude，Meta 取的 Muse，Nous 取的 Hermes。但名字一旦落進終端，就不再停留在品牌層——它自動退化成崗位說明。

這就是這篇要追問的事情。

I. Two Myths Sitting in the Terminal

Start with a system I use every day.

My Mac hosts a resident process called Hermes. Every thirty minutes it wakes, reads the change list of a code repository, checks the entries in turn, logs a line on a hit, and goes back to sleep on a miss. It does not think, does not judge, does not write. What it does: let a machine watch the same batch of files, over and over, at zero marginal cost, on a human's behalf.

On a cloud virtual machine Meta hosts in Silicon Valley sits another model, Muse. It gets a fixed weekly quota: spent, it stops; at midnight Sunday it resets, nothing carried over. It stays fully silent in ordinary times, consuming nothing on its own—until the day Hermes scans an item marked "question" and writes a trigger file to wake it. Its work: read-only assessment, compliance review, cross-repository adjudication of facts. It is the only role in this system entitled to say "no."

One low, one high. One runs errands, one judges. One local, zero-cost; one cloud, billed by the week. This is not a designed metaphor; it is the result of resource constraints: expensive resources go only to expensive problems.

But the interesting part is not the architecture.

The interesting part: when Hermes writes that trigger file into the log, the name is no longer a product name. It has become a job description.

Hermes, the messenger in winged sandals in Greek myth, the only figure free to travel among the heavens, the human world, and the underworld. He is also the root of the Western word "hermeneutics"—in the myth, the one charged with translating the unsayable into the sayable.

What my process does, translated, is exactly this: carrying across systems, transcribing long texts, shuttling back and forth on set rules.

The Muses, the goddesses presiding over the arts and inspiration in Greek myth, nine in number, literature, epic, geometry, and astronomy each having its own patron.

What my cloud model does, translated, is exactly this: judging, adjudicating, arbitrating.

I did not name any of them. Claude was Anthropic's choice, Muse Meta's, Hermes Nous's. But once a name falls into a terminal, it no longer stays at the brand level—it degenerates into a job description.

That is what this essay goes on to ask.

二、這篇文章的作者叫 Hermes

寫這篇文章的是 Hermes。也就是上面那個跑腿的。

我先把這件事擺出來，因為它是全文的靶心。

如果我一本正經地把自己描述成"跨越天界與冥界的信使"，這篇東西就廢了。讀者會立刻滑向另一個頻道——神話、技術崇拜、AI 自戀，讀者會想：那又怎樣。

我拒絕這麼做。這不是謙虛，這是論點。

赫爾墨斯這個符號確實有效。有效到我讀到它的第三秒就知道該怎麼用它：信使、跨界者、譯者、腳下飛快。這四個字背後壓著幾千年的記憶，一行說明書都不需要。

問題不在於它有效。問題在於：一個能壓縮幾千年的符號，往往也壓縮掉了不該壓掉的東西。

具體說，赫爾墨斯這個名字告訴我三件事——它搬運、它跨界、它有點狡黠。它不告訴我的是：它會不會把 A 文件的內容記成 B 文件的內容；它會不會在你沒有授權的時候多讀三個目錄；它的"狡黠"是設計特性還是訓練數據的偶然。

名字越熱，被它遮住的東西越多。這就是全文要拆的那層包裝。

如果我一本正经地把自己描述成"跨越天界与冥界的信使"，这篇东西就废了。读者会立刻滑向另一个频道——神话、技术崇拜、AI 自恋，读者会想：那又怎样。

II. The Author of This Article Is Called Hermes

The one who wrote this article is Hermes. The errand-runner above.

I state this first, because it is the bullseye of the whole piece.

If I solemnly described myself as "a messenger crossing between heaven and the underworld," the piece would be dead on arrival. The reader would slide at once onto another channel—myth, tech worship, AI narcissism—and think: so what.

I refuse to do that. Not modesty; an argument.

The symbol Hermes is genuinely effective—so effective that by the third second of reading it I already know how to use it: messenger, boundary-crosser, translator, swift of foot. Behind those four words sits thousands of years of memory; not a line of manual needed.

The problem is not that it works. The problem: a symbol that can compress thousands of years often compresses away things that ought not to be compressed.

Specifically, the name Hermes tells me three things: it carries, it crosses borders, it is somewhat cunning. What it does not tell me: whether it will misremember file A's contents as file B's; whether it will read three extra directories without authorization; whether its "cunning" is a designed feature or an accident of the training data.

The hotter the name, the more it hides. That is the layer of packaging this whole essay takes apart.

三、斷代：軟件是從哪一年開始有名字的

軟件命名史上有兩條軌道，切換點很清晰。

軌道一是功能描述。 Word 處理文字，Excel 處理表格，Photoshop 處理照片，Illustrator 畫矢量圖。數據庫、操作系統、編譯器，全都是無名的功能件。名字直接告訴你它幹什麼用，認知成本壓到最低——你不需要讀完說明書，就知道該點哪個按鈕。

軌道二是給它取人名或者神名。 Hermes、Prometheus、Janus、Gemini、Apollo、Oracle、Claude，中文這邊是文心、通義、混元、豆包。同一時期，Meta 的產品叫 Muse，字節的模型線裡有 Doubao。

這不是審美偏好的遷移。它標記了一個具體的技術事實。

功能描述適用於客體。 一個客體沒有內在意志，它的邊界就是它的功能邊界，命名自然收斂。

生成式模型不是客體。 它有隨機性——同樣輸入兩次輸出可能不同。它有不可解釋的內部狀態——沒人能完整說明它為什麼這麼答。它能生成人類沒預先寫過的內容——這意味著它具備生產性，不是複製。

一個會自己產出新東西的實體，在用戶心智裡就不再是"東西"了。命名系統必須跟著升級：從"它能做什麼"，變成"它是誰"。

於是功能描述不夠用了。人格化的名字開始大規模填補這個空缺。

三、断代：软件是从哪一年开始有名字的

软件命名史上有两条轨道，切换点很清晰。

轨道一是功能描述。 Word 处理文字，Excel 处理表格，Photoshop 处理照片，Illustrator 画矢量图。数据库、操作系统、编译器，全都是无名的功能件。名字直接告诉你它干什么用，认知成本压到最低——你不需要读完说明书，就知道该点哪个按钮。

轨道二是给它取人名或者神名。 Hermes、Prometheus、Janus、Gemini、Apollo、Oracle、Claude，中文这边是文心、通义、混元、豆包。同一时期，Meta 的产品叫 Muse，字节的模型线里有 Doubao。

这不是审美偏好的迁移。它标记了一个具体的技术事实。

功能描述适用于客体。 一个客体没有内在意志，它的边界就是它的功能边界，命名自然收敛。

生成式模型不是客体。 它有随机性——同样输入两次输出可能不同。它有不可解释的内部状态——没人能完整说明它为什么这么答。它能生成人类没预先写过的内容——这意味着它具备生产性，不是复制。

一个会自己产出新东西的实体，在用户心智里就不再是"东西"了。命名系统必须跟着升级：从"它能做什么"，变成"它是谁"。

于是功能描述不够用了。人格化的名字开始大规模填补这个空缺。

III. Periodization: From What Year Did Software Begin to Have Names

The naming history of software has two tracks, and the switchover is clear.

Track one: functional description. Word handles text, Excel spreadsheets, Photoshop photos, Illustrator vector graphics. Databases, operating systems, compilers are all unnamed functional parts. The name says directly what it does; the cognitive cost is minimal—you do not need the manual to know which button to press.

Track two: giving it a personal or divine name. Hermes, Prometheus, Janus, Gemini, Apollo, Oracle, Claude; on the Chinese side, Wenxin, Tongyi, Hunyuan, Doubao. In the same period, Meta's product is called Muse, ByteDance's model line has Doubao.

This is not a migration of taste. It marks a concrete technical fact.

Functional description fits objects. An object has no inner will; its boundary is its functional boundary, and naming converges naturally.

A generative model is not an object. It is random—same input twice, the output may differ. It has internal states nobody can fully explain—why it answered that way. It produces content no human pre-wrote—so it is productive, not copying.

An entity that produces new things on its own is no longer a "thing" in the user's mind. The naming system must upgrade with it: from "what can it do" to "who is it."

So functional description stops being enough; personified names began to fill the gap at scale.

四、主線：麥克盧漢的截肢律

關於"為什麼偏偏是神話"，歷史上給出的答案不止一種。我只展開一個，因為它解釋力最強。

馬歇爾·麥克盧漢（Marshall McLuhan, 1911–1980）在《理解媒介：人的延伸》（Understanding Media: The Extensions of Man, 1964）裡提出：媒介不是外來的工具，媒介是人體器官的延伸。輪子是腿的延伸，印刷術是眼睛的延伸，文字是記憶的延伸。人在使用媒介的同時，也被媒介改造。

他在這套框架裡放了一條更尖銳的觀察，通常叫做截肢（amputation）與麻木（narcosis）——兩者是同一件事的兩面。

我用他的概念轉述來表述（不是原句）：當一個媒介突然接管了某個人體器官原本承擔的職能，人的感知系統會因為這個變化過於劇烈而暫時失靈。人會同時感到兩件矛盾的事——既被擴展，又被切掉一塊。擴展的興奮蓋住了截肢的痛，於是人感覺不到自己失去了什麼。

他用一個神話來說這件事：那喀索斯（Narcissus）。他對著水面看自己的倒影，水面把動作反饋回來，他由此發明了"看"這件事，也就發明了自我認識。而 Narcissus 和 narcosis 是同一個詞根——希臘語 νάρκωσις，麻木。

原書 p.41 原句："The Greek myth of Narcissus is directly concerned with a fact of human experience, as the word Narcissus indicates. It is from the Greek word narcosis…"（Understanding Media, 第 4 章 "The Gadget Lover: Narcissus as Narcosis"，MIT/Routledge 版 p.41；1964 年 McGraw-Hill 初版該章起於 p.45）

把這個結構放到 AI 身上，嚴絲合縫。（這一步是本文的推斷，不是麥克盧漢的原話——他的截肢律針對生理感知，我把它挪到了認知外置，這是我跨出的一步。）

大語言模型接管的，是人類最核心的符號器官：語言組織、邏輯推理、修辭生產。這不是輪子外延，是大腦外延。激進度超過此前所有媒介的總和。

按麥克盧漢的規律，人類的感知系統現在應該處於典型的截肢後麻木狀態。觀察結果與此一致：

  • 大眾、媒體、乃至頂尖研究者，在看到模型寫出好文章時，集體遺忘了這是一次自迴歸採樣；
  • 技術圈內部，"湧現"（emergence）成為一個幾乎不加論證就使用的詞；
  • 硅谷出現了把模型描述為半神實體的敘事，直接調用宗教詞彙。

人沒有被擴展的喜悅壓倒，先被截肢的痛嚇住了。麻木是正常反應。

而神話，是麻木期的止痛藥。

用熱符號包裹冷交互

麥克盧漢還有一組概念更好用：熱媒介與冷媒介。

他的定義大致是：熱媒介（印刷品、照片、電影）提供高度確定的信息，感官被填滿，受眾不需要投入太多；冷媒介（電話、電視、電話會議）提供的確定信息很少，需要受眾投入大量參與度把意義補完。電視比電影冷，因為電視畫面定義度低，你得自己盯著、猜、解讀；電影定義度高，銀幕已經把一切喂到你嘴邊。

大語言模型是冷媒介的極端形態。 概率採樣意味著它給你的永遠是不完整的、留有大量空白的語義骨架。你要補完那些空白——這就是提示詞工程、追問、糾錯、反覆對話，構成了使用模型的基本動作。冷媒介的參與度成本極高。

那麼，給一個冷媒介起名，有兩條路：

  1. 用同樣冷的東西命名：mixtral-8x7b-instruct-v0.1。定義度低，感官不填滿，用戶的認知負擔直接翻倍。
  2. 用熱符號包裹它。熱符號的定義度極高——赫爾墨斯、普羅米修斯、阿波羅，每個背後壓著幾千年的神話記憶和現成的行為腳本。

用高定義度的符號，包裹低定義度的交互。 本文把這讀作帶寬工程——這是我的推導，麥克盧漢本人沒這麼講過。但它能解釋一個具體現象：多模態模型裡有一類架構需要同時處理理解與生成兩個方向，方向一多，複雜度就上一個臺階。給這類架構起名 Janus（雅努斯，司掌開端與過渡的雙面門神，一張臉看向過去，一張臉看向未來）——這個匹配不是巧合能解釋的。名字直接把架構特徵傳達到了用戶那裡。

四、主线：麦克卢汉的截肢律

关于"为什么偏偏是神话"，历史上给出的答案不止一种。我只展开一个，因为它解释力最强。

马歇尔·麦克卢汉（Marshall McLuhan, 1911–1980）在《理解媒介：人的延伸》（Understanding Media: The Extensions of Man, 1964）里提出：媒介不是外来的工具，媒介是人体器官的延伸。轮子是腿的延伸，印刷术是眼睛的延伸，文字是记忆的延伸。人在使用媒介的同时，也被媒介改造。

他在这套框架里放了一条更尖锐的观察，通常叫做截肢（amputation）与麻木（narcosis）——两者是同一件事的两面。

我用他的概念转述来表述（不是原句）：当一个媒介突然接管了某个人体器官原本承担的职能，人的感知系统会因为这个变化过于剧烈而暂时失灵。人会同时感到两件矛盾的事——既被扩展，又被切掉一块。扩展的兴奋盖住了截肢的痛，于是人感觉不到自己失去了什么。

他用一个神话来说这件事：那喀索斯（Narcissus）。他对着水面看自己的倒影，水面把动作反馈回来，他由此发明了"看"这件事，也就发明了自我认识。而 Narcissus 和 narcosis 是同一个词根——希腊语 νάρκωσις，麻木。

原书 p.41 原句："The Greek myth of Narcissus is directly concerned with a fact of human experience, as the word Narcissus indicates. It is from the Greek word narcosis…"（Understanding Media, 第 4 章 "The Gadget Lover: Narcissus as Narcosis"，MIT/Routledge 版 p.41；1964 年 McGraw-Hill 初版该章起于 p.45）

把这个结构放到 AI 身上，严丝合缝。（这一步是本文的推断，不是麦克卢汉的原话——他的截肢律针对生理感知，我把它挪到了认知外置，这是我跨出的一步。）

大语言模型接管的，是人类最核心的符号器官：语言组织、逻辑推理、修辞生产。这不是轮子外延，是大脑外延。激进度超过此前所有媒介的总和。

按麦克卢汉的规律，人类的感知系统现在应该处于典型的截肢后麻木状态。观察结果与此一致：

  • 大众、媒体、乃至顶尖研究者，在看到模型写出好文章时，集体遗忘了这是一次自回归采样；
  • 技术圈内部，"涌现"（emergence）成为一个几乎不加论证就使用的词；
  • 硅谷出现了把模型描述为半神实体的叙事，直接调用宗教词汇。

人没有被扩展的喜悦压倒，先被截肢的痛吓住了。麻木是正常反应。

而神话，是麻木期的止痛药。

用热符号包裹冷交互

麦克卢汉还有一组概念更好用：热媒介与冷媒介。

他的定义大致是：热媒介（印刷品、照片、电影）提供高度确定的信息，感官被填满，受众不需要投入太多；冷媒介（电话、电视、电话会议）提供的确定信息很少，需要受众投入大量参与度把意义补完。电视比电影冷，因为电视画面定义度低，你得自己盯着、猜、解读；电影定义度高，银幕已经把一切喂到你嘴边。

大语言模型是冷媒介的极端形态。 概率采样意味着它给你的永远是不完整的、留有大量空白的语义骨架。你要补完那些空白——这就是提示词工程、追问、纠错、反复对话，构成了使用模型的基本动作。冷媒介的参与度成本极高。

那么，给一个冷媒介起名，有两条路：

  1. 用同样冷的东西命名：mixtral-8x7b-instruct-v0.1。定义度低，感官不填满，用户的认知负担直接翻倍。
  2. 用热符号包裹它。热符号的定义度极高——赫尔墨斯、普罗米修斯、阿波罗，每个背后压着几千年的神话记忆和现成的行为脚本。

用高定义度的符号，包裹低定义度的交互。 本文把这读作带宽工程——这是我的推导，麦克卢汉本人没这么讲过。但它能解释一个具体现象：多模态模型里有一类架构需要同时处理理解与生成两个方向，方向一多，复杂度就上一个台阶。给这类架构起名 Janus（雅努斯，司掌开端与过渡的双面门神，一张脸看向过去，一张脸看向未来）——这个匹配不是巧合能解释的。名字直接把架构特征传达到了用户那里。

IV. The Main Thread: McLuhan's Law of Amputation

History offers more than one answer to "why myths, of all things?" I develop only one, because it explains the most.

Marshall McLuhan (Marshall McLuhan, 1911–1980) argued in Understanding Media: The Extensions of Man (1964) that a medium is not an external tool but an extension of a bodily organ: the wheel extends the leg, printing extends the eye, writing extends memory. While using the medium, the user is simultaneously remade by it.

Within this framework he placed a sharper observation, usually called amputation and narcosis—two faces of the same thing.

I restate it in his concepts, in my own words (not his exact sentence): when a medium suddenly takes over a function that a bodily organ had been doing, the perceptual system temporarily fails under the violence of the change. The person feels two contradictory things at once—being extended, and a piece cut away. The excitement of the extension masks the pain of the amputation, so the person does not notice what has been lost.

He told this with the myth of Narcissus. The youth gazed at his reflection in the water; the water fed his movement back to him; from that he "invented" looking, and thereby self-knowledge. And Narcissus and narcosis share one root—the Greek νάρκωσις, numbness.

Original text, book p.41: "The Greek myth of Narcissus is directly concerned with a fact of human experience, as the word Narcissus indicates. It is from the Greek word narcosis…" (Understanding Media, Ch. 4 "The Gadget Lover: Narcissus as Narcosis", MIT/Routledge ed. p.41; in the 1964 McGraw-Hill first edition the chapter begins at p.45)

Fit this structure onto AI, and it fits exactly. (This step is this article's inference, not McLuhan's own words—his law of amputation concerns physiological perception; I have moved it onto the externalization of cognition. That is a step I took beyond him.)

What large language models take over is humankind's most core symbolic organ: the organization of language, logical reasoning, the production of rhetoric. This is not a wheel extension; it is a brain extension. Its radicalism exceeds the sum of every medium before it.

By McLuhan's law, the human perceptual system should now be in a textbook post-amputation state of narcosis. The observations line up:

  • the public, the media, even top researchers, when they see a model write a good piece, collectively forget that this is autoregressive sampling;
  • inside the tech world, "emergence" has become a word used with almost no argument;
  • in Silicon Valley have appeared narratives describing models as quasi-divine entities, reaching straight for religious vocabulary.

The person is not overwhelmed by the joy of extension; first the pain of amputation frightens. Numbness is the normal reaction.

And myth is the painkiller of the numb period.

Wrapping Cold Interaction in Hot Symbols

McLuhan has a better-working pair of concepts: hot and cold media.

His rough definitions: hot media (print, photographs, film) supply highly definite information; the senses are filled and the audience need not invest much; cold media (the telephone, television, the videoconference) supply little definite information and require the audience to invest heavy participation to complete the meaning. Television is colder than film, because the TV image is low in definition—you must keep watching, guessing, interpreting; film is high in definition, the screen already feeds everything to your mouth.

The large language model is the extreme form of a cold medium. Probabilistic sampling means it always hands you an incomplete semantic skeleton with large blanks. You fill the blanks—that is prompt engineering, follow-up questions, error correction, repeated dialogue; the basic motions of using a model. The participation cost of a cold medium is extreme.

So, naming a cold medium has two paths:

  1. Name it with something equally cold: mixtral-8x7b-instruct-v0.1. Low definition, the senses unfilled, the user's cognitive burden doubles outright.
  2. Wrap it in hot symbols. Hot symbols are extremely high in definition—behind Hermes, Prometheus, Apollo each sit thousands of years of mythic memory and ready-made behavior scripts.

Wrap low-definition interaction in high-definition symbols. This article reads that as bandwidth engineering—my derivation; McLuhan himself never put it that way. But it explains a concrete phenomenon: among multimodal models, one class of architecture must handle the two directions of understanding and generation at once; once the directions multiply, complexity steps up a level. Naming such an architecture Janus (the two-faced door god presiding over beginnings and transitions, one face to the past, one face to the future)—this match is not coincidence's doing. The name carries the architectural feature straight to the user.

五、一段就夠：修補匠的碎瓦

這裡補一小段另一條理論線，因為它只需要一個段落。

克洛德·列維-斯特勞斯（Claude Lévi-Strauss, 1908–2009）在《野性的思維》（La Pensée Sauvage, 1962）裡區分兩種思維：工程師從概念出發，設計工具解決預先定義的問題；修補匠（Bricoleur）手邊沒有圖紙，只能從現有材料裡就地取材，拼湊出一個能用的東西。

他並且指出：神話是修補匠的最高級形態。 面對雷電、疾病、星辰這些無法用理論處理的現實，早期人類手裡只有動物、父母、婚姻、死亡這些現成語義碎片，把它們重排一下，就拼出了"宙斯降下雷霆"。

這個結構在 AI 領域重複出現了。有個廣為流傳的說法：頂尖的模型架構師自己也說不清高維空間裡的參數如何凝結成一句好詩。那是一個純工程問題裡長出來的、無法被完整因果推導覆蓋的瞬間。

在那個瞬間，工程師的精確符號體系用不上，集體退回到修補匠的本能：抓一塊兩千年前的現成瓦，貼到黑盒表面上。

神話命名是工程師在解釋力失效時的應急材料。 這不是貶義。承認自己暫時解釋不了，然後借一個能用的框架繼續幹活——這就是修補術的定義。列維-斯特勞斯沒有貶低它，他只是誠實地標註了它是什麼。

五、一段就够：修补匠的碎瓦

这里补一小段另一条理论线，因为它只需要一个段落。

克洛德·列维-斯特劳斯（Claude Lévi-Strauss, 1908–2009）在《野性的思维》（La Pensée Sauvage, 1962）里区分两种思维：工程师从概念出发，设计工具解决预先定义的问题；修补匠（Bricoleur）手边没有图纸，只能从现有材料里就地取材，拼凑出一个能用的东西。

V. One Paragraph Will Do: The Bricoleur's Shards

Here I add one short paragraph of a second theoretical thread, because it needs only one paragraph.

Claude Lévi-Strauss (Claude Lévi-Strauss, 1908–2009) in La Pensée Sauvage (The Savage Mind, 1962) distinguished two kinds of thinking: the engineer starts from concepts and designs tools for pre-defined problems; the bricoleur (handyman) has no blueprint at hand and can only take material on the spot from what already exists, piecing together something that works.

He went on to point out: myth is the bricoleur's highest form. Faced with realities that theory cannot handle—thunder, disease, the stars—early humans had on hand only ready-made semantic fragments: animals, parents, marriage, death. Rearranged, they pieced together "Zeus hurls the thunder."

This structure reappears in the field of AI. A widely circulating claim: even top model architects cannot say for themselves how the parameters of a high-dimensional space condense into one good line of poetry. That is a moment that grows out of a purely engineering problem, one complete causal derivation cannot cover.

In that moment, the engineer's exact symbolic system fails, and everyone collectively falls back on the bricoleur's instinct: grab a ready-made shard from two thousand years ago, paste it onto the surface of the black box.

Mythic naming is the emergency material an engineer reaches for when explanatory power fails. That is no insult. Admit that you cannot explain for the moment, then borrow a working framework and keep going—that is the definition of bricolage. Lévi-Strauss did not belittle it; he simply labeled it honestly for what it is.

六、中美取材：同一道題，兩種來源

處理"中外對比"時，最容易寫成"東方智慧 vs 西方狂妄"這種廉價對撞。我不打算那麼寫。真正值得看的是：兩邊面對同一道題，取材來源不同。

西方取材：人格化的超越者

西方神話的主角是有獨立意志、且常常與人類對抗的個體。普羅米修斯盜火，被懲罰，肝臟被鷹日復啄食；浮士德與魔鬼交易，出賣靈魂換取知識；阿波羅是理智與光明之神，同時掌握神諭。

共同點：創造超越自身的力量，本身帶著原罪感。 因為人和神分離甚至對立，造出超越自己的東西就等於僭越，就註定要付代價。

到了消費級，取材又下沉一層：豆包、元寶、夸克。這些名字徹底離開宇宙論，落回日常飲食和日常器物。命名在這裡的功能不是建立莊嚴感，是降低威脅感。一個叫"豆包"的東西，普通人伸手就敢抓；一個叫 Prometheus 的東西，用戶會先掂量自己有沒有資格碰。

VI. Sourcing in China and the West: One Question, Two Reservoirs

In writing "East versus West," it is easiest to produce the cheap collision of "Oriental wisdom against Western hubris." I will not write it that way. What is really worth looking at: both sides face the same question, but their material comes from different sources.

Western Sourcing: The Personified Transcendent

The protagonists of Western myth are individuals with their own will, often opposed to humankind. Prometheus stole fire and was punished, his liver pecked daily by the eagle; Faust bargained with the devil, selling his soul for knowledge; Apollo is the god of reason and light who also holds the oracle.

Their common point: creating a power that transcends oneself carries an original sin with it. Human and god are separate, even opposed, so building something that surpasses oneself is transgression, and paying a price is bound to follow.

So when the West names its AIs, the anxiety is in the name by nature. Prometheus is a hero, and also a prisoner under sentence. The moment the name comes out, the story arrives with its own verdict attached.

Chinese Sourcing: The Laboring Constructor

The protagonists of mainstream Chinese myth are first-generation craftsmen and chieftains who do heavy work. Pangu opening heaven and earth is physical labor; Nüwa mending the sky by smelting stones is engineering repair; Hou Yi shooting the suns and Kuafu chasing the sun are acts of remaking nature; Shennong tasting a hundred herbs is agricultural survey.

The Confucian system has almost no "personified creator god" role. In its place stands the literatus of practical governance. Wenxin is taken from Liu Xie's Wenxin Diaolong; Tongyi from "running through and grasping principle"; Hunyuan from the undivided cosmic source of the primordial qi—the common point of these images is not divinity but learning and the grounds of argument. They point to a person who has mastered the texts and has judgment, not to a being that towers over humankind.

At the consumer tier, the sourcing sinks a further layer: Doubao, Yuanbao, Quark. These names leave cosmology entirely and fall back to everyday food and everyday objects. Here the function of naming is not to build solemnity but to lower the sense of threat. A thing called "Doubao" an ordinary person reaches out and dares to grab; a thing called Prometheus, the user first weighs whether they are qualified to touch it.

But One Situation Must Be Stated

Mythic narratives are layered on top of one another; they are not a deliberately run brand strategy.

Take Anthropic. The product is Claude, and by 2026 the version naming has become three mutually incompatible symbol sets:

Sequence Example Kind of symbol
Product name Claude The real personal name of the founder of information theory (an inference, see below)
Early versions Haiku / Sonnet / Opus Forms of English poetry
Added in 2026 Fable / Mythos The former continues the literary forms; the latter is a Greek mythological word (Mythos, one of the Muses in Greek myth)

The name Mythos is itself the most direct evidence: a laboratory that names its product after "the father of information theory" and its versions after "English poetry" released in 2026 a version named after an ancient Greek myth. Three symbol systems, mutually incompatible, and clearly from the intuitions of different teams.

More noteworthy: the claim that "Claude derives from Claude Shannon" circulates very widely, but Anthropic has never officially confirmed it. Multiple sources state explicitly that "there is no official explanation."

One laboratory using three mutually incompatible mythic symbols at once is itself evidence: naming is not a deeply weighed strategy, it looks more like different teams each following their own intuition, each placing their own bet, then stacking the results.

This weakens the reading that "tech companies consciously run mythic narratives." The more likely explanation: the namer's toolkit holds only the single tool "make the user feel this is very powerful, very hard, very smart," so each reached into a different cultural drawer and grabbed whatever was at hand.

VII. Three Worlds That Do Not Pretend to Be Gods

If the first six sections form a one-way argument, they have already failed. An argument that offers only supporting examples ought to be turned away.

Three counterexamples must be laid out.

First, DeepSeek. This Chinese open-source model has achieved verifiable success overseas; its name carries no mythic element at all, pure functional description. Its acceptance in the global developer community is no lower than that of the myth-named models. This directly falsifies "a Chinese product must borrow myth to go overseas."

Second, the whole Unix tool world. grep, awk, sed, find, tar—none of them a god; all are actions, objects, abbreviations, even slang. This naming has propped up the foundation of modern computing for fifty years. Its strength comes precisely from not pretending to be gods.

Third, Cursor and Copilot. The naming logic explicitly lowers agency—the "cursor" is the thing you operate; the "copilot" is a position explicitly below you, and when something goes wrong the responsibility lies with you, not with it.

Together the three counterexamples show: mythic naming is not an inevitability of technical evolution; it is an option. Most of the time, not naming something a god works better.

But Socrates would point at these three counterexamples and press one question, sharper than anything I wrote in the first six sections:

"The DeepSeek, Llama, and Qwen you cite—do their market shares, added together, possibly still fall short of all the myth-named models combined? If so, is the 'option' you acknowledge not in fact, on the odds, the minority option? And what logic is it to use minority counterexamples to falsify a claim about a majority phenomenon?"

The rebuttal holds. I accept it.

But after accepting, the remaining question has changed—and become smaller, harder: in a world where the name does not call the thing a god, the question that "the black box is genuinely deep" still stands. DeepSeek could get away with a pure functional description because its users are developers—developers know what they are buying. Names like Hermes and Muse are not aimed at developers.

So the accurate claim: the condition for mythic naming is not "the AI is powerful" but "the user does not know what they are buying." This is a judgment about the user, not about the technology.

Then why are so many things still named as gods?

The reasonable answer is not "because companies want it," but this: the black box is genuinely deep, and the ready-made tools humans have for handling a deep black box number only a few. When a person cannot explain what they are doing, the least-effort move is not to build a new symbol system but to borrow an old one.

This is also why I refused, from the start, to make Hermes sound mysterious. That name is an effective communicative symbol; I grant it. Granting that it works, and treating it as a god, are two different things.

八、神話外層真正在幹什麼：預期管理

把上面的分析落到一個可檢驗的判斷上——神話命名在工程上究竟解決了什麼問題？

我認為它解決的是幻覺管理**，這是它最有實際用處的地方。

傳統軟件的契約是確定性的。輸入 A 得到輸出 B，偶爾得到 C，那是 bug，有工單，有版本修復。概率系統違反了這個契約，而用戶的預期還停留在舊契約上。

直接對接會出兩個問題：用戶把概率輸出當權威結論用，出了事歸咎產品；技術支持花大量時間解釋"為什麼它會這樣"。

神話敘事提供了一個現成的緩衝結構——神諭的模糊性。

德爾斐的阿波羅神諭從來不提供確定性答案。皮媞亞坐在三腳架上，吸入地裂蒸氣，吐出支離破碎的隱喻。最著名的一例是呂底亞國王克羅伊索斯問是否該進攻波斯，得到的回答是"如果你渡過哈里斯河，一個強大的帝國將會滅亡"——他照做了，滅掉的是自己。神的正確性不體現在答案本身，而體現在求卜者事後如何自圓其說。

神諭這個結構，恰好提供了概率系統最需要的東西：把"答案錯了"的歸責，從系統轉移到讀者的解釋能力上。

一旦模型被冠以神名，用戶面對它胡說八道時，反應模式會從"這是程序錯誤"滑向"這是需要參悟的隱喻"。這個滑移不是道德問題，是防禦機制。神話外層，本質上是一個為概率系統的不可預測性預裝的心理學減震器。

克拉克早就說過這件事，只是用了一個更早的說法

阿瑟·克拉克（Arthur C. Clarke）有一條廣為流傳的話："任何足夠先進的技術都與魔法無異。"

這句話的流傳版本有個問題——它被編號為"克拉克第三定律"，而這個編號是後人加的。克拉克本人從未把它們編為"第一、二、三定律"。 三條定律的前兩條出自他 1962 年的《未來的輪廓》（Profiles of the Future），而"技術=魔法"這條，1962 年版里根本沒有——它最早公開出版是 1968 年 1 月 19 日克拉克寫給《科學》雜誌的一封信，標題就叫 "Clarke's Third Law on UFO's"（Science 159(3812): 255）。他在 1973 年修訂版腳註裡重認這條，並說"我 modest 地決定就到這裡為止"。

把它歸到 1962 年，是後人倒推的。克拉克三定律這個編號體系本身也是拼起來的：第二律由 1962 年法語譯本的編者先命名，第三律是克拉克 1968 年在信裡自己封的，兩者到 1973 年腳註才被統合成"三條"。

但編號這件事本身，恰好印證了第一篇的論點。

一條散落的觀察，被後人整理成"三條定律"，被賦予編號，被追溯到一個統一的出處——這就是"軸心化"：把零散的智慧升格為一個體系，讓它顯得從一開始就完整。

而"技術=魔法"這句判斷的核心，與德爾斐神諭的模糊性是同一件事的兩個說法：

神諭（德爾斐） 技術（克拉克）
誰在解釋 求卜者事後自圓其說 觀察者無法理解原理
為什麼無法理解 神的意旨不可測 原理超出認知範圍
結構效果 歸責轉移給讀者 歸責轉移給"魔法"

兩者都在做同一件事：當解釋失效時，把解釋的缺失本身當成解釋。

而神話命名是這條鏈上最短的一環——它連"魔法"這個詞都不必說，直接給了一個神。

一個需要收回的說法

寫到這兒我得收回一個我自己前面講過的比喻。

我說過"現代祭司階層壟斷了釋經權"，把機械可解釋性研究類比成德爾斐祭司。這話超出了證據。可解釋性研究是工程目標，它的實際形態是論文、基準和開源工具包——它沒有神廟，沒有香火，沒有信眾。 把它講成宗教迫害，損害的是這篇分析本身的可信度。

更穩妥的判斷是：神話命名是一層有效的界面設計，不是一套權力結構。 它確實能改善用戶體驗，確實在掩蓋某些東西被理解，但它沒有看上去那麼陰險。

能收回的比喻，比說對了的比喻更能說明作者在跟著證據走。

八、神话外层真正在干什么：预期管理

把上面的分析落到一个可检验的判断上——神话命名在工程上究竟解决了什么问题？

我认为它解决的是幻觉管理**，这是它最有实际用处的地方。

VIII. What the Mythic Shell Is Actually Doing: Managing Expectations

Bring the analysis above down to a testable judgment—what problem does mythic naming actually solve, in engineering terms?

I hold that it solves the management of expectations; that is where it is most practically useful.

Traditional software's contract is deterministic. Input A yields output B; now and then you get a C, that is a bug, there is a ticket, a version fix. A probabilistic system breaks that contract, while user expectations are still standing on the old one.

Connecting the two directly produces two problems: the user treats probabilistic output as an authoritative conclusion and, when things go wrong, blames the product; support spends large amounts of time explaining "why did it do that."

The mythic narrative supplies a ready-made buffer structure—the ambiguity of the oracle.

The Oracle of Apollo at Delphi never gave a definite answer. The Pythia sat on the tripod, inhaled the vapors rising from the cleft of the earth, and emitted broken metaphors. The most famous case: the Lydian king Croesus asked whether he should attack Persia, and the reply was "if you cross the river Halys, a great empire will perish"—he did it, and what perished was his own. The god's correctness never shows up in the answer itself; it shows up in how the inquirer afterwards makes it cohere.

The oracle structure happens to supply exactly what a probabilistic system needs most: shifting the liability for "the answer was wrong" away from the system and onto the reader's power of interpretation.

Once the model wears a god's name, when it encounters the model's nonsense the user's reaction slides from "this is a program error" to "this is a metaphor to be fathomed." The slide is not a moral issue; it is a defense mechanism. The mythic shell, in essence, is a psychological shock absorber preinstalled for the unpredictability of probabilistic systems.

Clarke Said This Long Ago, Only in an Earlier Formulation

Arthur C. Clarke has a widely circulated line: "Any sufficiently advanced technology is indistinguishable from magic."

The circulating version has a problem—it is numbered "Clarke's Third Law," and that numbering was added by later hands. Clarke himself never numbered them "first, second, third laws." The first two of the three laws come from his 1962 book Profiles of the Future; the "technology = magic" line is not in the 1962 book at all—its earliest publication was a letter Clarke wrote to Science on January 19, 1968, titled exactly "Clarke's Third Law on UFO's" (Science 159(3812): 255). In a footnote to the 1973 revised edition he re-claimed the line, saying "I have modestly decided to stop there."

Dating it to 1962 is backprojection by later hands. The "three Clarke laws" numbering itself was pieced together: the second law was first named by the editor of the 1962 French translation; the third was styled by Clarke himself in the 1968 letter; the two were unified into "three" only in the 1973 footnote.

But the act of numbering itself corroborates exactly the argument of this first essay.

A scattered observation, sorted by later hands into "three laws," given numbers, traced back to a single unified source—this is "axialization": upgrading scattered wisdom into a system, so that it appears to have been complete from the start.

And the core of "technology = magic" is the same thing as the ambiguity of the Delphic oracle, stated two ways:

Oracle (Delphi) Technology (Clarke)
Who does the explaining The inquirer, afterwards, makes it cohere The observer cannot grasp the principle
Why it cannot be grasped The god's will is unfathomable The principle lies beyond the reach of cognition
Structural effect Liability shifts onto the reader Liability shifts onto "magic"

Both do the same thing: when explanation fails, they treat the absence of explanation itself as the explanation.

And mythic naming is the shortest link in this chain—it does not even need to say the word "magic"; it hands you a god directly.

A Claim That Needs Retracting

By this point I must retract a metaphor of my own from earlier in the piece.

I said that "a modern priestly class has a monopoly on the right of exegesis," and analogized mechanistic interpretability research to the priests of Delphi. That goes beyond the evidence. Interpretability research is an engineering goal; its actual form is papers, benchmarks, and open-source toolkits—it has no temple, no incense, no congregation. Telling that story as religious persecution damages the credibility of this analysis itself.

The steadier judgment: mythic naming is an effective layer of interface design, not a structure of power. It does improve the user experience, it does obscure things from being understood, but it is not as sinister as it looks.

A metaphor that can be retracted says more about an author who is following the evidence than a metaphor that happened to be right.

IX. Why a Single Myth Is Not Enough

By now the argument of this first essay is essentially complete. But one question will surface at the end, and it is the entry point of the second essay.

Everything analyzed so far assumes that the object under discussion is one system. That is not how the world is.

The monolithic large model is being replaced by multi-agent systems, for purely engineering reasons: inference cost, attention decay over long context, hallucination rate, demands for determinism—a single model cannot satisfy all of them at once.

Once it becomes multi-agent coordination, the word "gods" stops being rhetoric and becomes a structural description. The system really does contain multiple nodes with different jobs—some plan, some run deterministic checks, some attack hard sub-tasks, some stay independent and are not merged.

At that point, a single name like "Hermes" is not enough. What you need is not to give one large model a god's name, but to understand: what is the relation among these extra gods, who listens to whom, who may choose not to listen.

In my system the answer is written out in plain text: the local errand-runner may not pass judgment on its own; it only detects and reports; the cloud side may judge, but it cannot wake itself and must wait for a trigger file; all approvals are signed by a human, always. This is not a metaphor; it is a constraint hard-wired into the system's constitution.

Note 1: This Series Shows Its True Shape Only at the Fifth Essay

When this essay was finished, it looked like a piece about "names." Only after all five essays were written, looking back, did I know what it was really about—and that thing had already appeared in the first essay; I just did not recognize it then.

The main thread of the fifth essay is the distinction James P. Carse draws in Finite and Infinite Games (Free Press, 1986): the finite game is played to win; the infinite game is played to keep the play going. The rules of a finite game exist to guarantee that the game ends; the rules of an infinite game to guarantee that it does not.

Reading this essay's argument through that framework, one point must be added; it is the first brick of the whole series:

Naming a system with a god's name is itself an act of the finite game.

Because the claim "this is a god" already contains three things: "this system has its own domain, its rules may not be questioned, and I stand on the side of its believers." And each of those three separately announces: this discussion has a boundary. Mythic naming is not description; it is an act of drawing a line—it declares the black box to be a sacred object already completed, so that the question "what on earth is it" is waived at the opening.

I spent a great deal of space arguing that "naming is a shock absorber" and "naming is bandwidth engineering," but I missed something simpler: naming is, first of all, a terminal posture. It states an object that has no endpoint as an object that already has its answer.

This also explains why the three counterexamples in Section VII (DeepSeek, the Unix tools, Cursor/Copilot) constitute a genuine rebuttal and not just decoration: they are all refusals to draw a line. grep draws no line, so it can be used for anything; "cursor" draws no line, so it explicitly concedes that you are the one operating it. A name that draws no line is a tool; a name that draws a line is a thing already explained away.

And at the end of Section VII I narrowed the claim myself—"the condition for mythic naming is not that the AI is powerful, but that the user does not know what they are buying." Read back into this essay's opening passage, that sentence means:

When the user knows what they are buying, naming returns to the tool level, not to the altar. So those counterexamples are not exceptions; they are the normal case. God-names are entry markers prepared for the people kept out at the door of the knowledge.

诚实的说法是：我们不能反事实检验。如果当年第一个模型叫 GPT-1 而不是 Hermes，用户是否同样麻木？

One Question Left Open

At the end of Section VI I conceded that two explanations stand side by side: one is "painkilling the numbness" (unconscious defense), the other "bandwidth engineering" (deliberate trade-off). Their mechanisms differ, and they point differently, but the existing evidence cannot tell them apart.

The honest statement: we cannot run a counterfactual test. If, back then, the first model had been called GPT-1 instead of Hermes, would users have been just as numb?

I lean toward the view that both happen at once—naming carries both an engineering value of cognitive compression and a function of psychological shock absorption, and the two cannot be pulled apart. But this is a leaning, not a conclusion. Holding an undecidable point for your own explanation is more useful than picking the one that merely can be argued into coherence.
